# Malaysia Budget 2027 individual income tax changes: legislative and PCB/MTD status (as of 6 November 2026)

All sources accessed 6 November 2026 unless stated otherwise. "Confirmed" means stated by a primary or reputable source found in this research. "Inferred" means my reasoning from the cited facts. Single-source claims are marked **[single source, lower confidence]**.

Short answer: as of 6 Nov 2026 the Budget 2027 individual tax changes (RM12,000 self relief and new Schedule 1 bands for YA2027) are **proposals, not law**. I found no evidence that the Finance Bill carrying them has been tabled yet. By precedent it would be tabled in mid-to-late November, passed by both Houses in December, and gazetted around 31 December 2026. LHDN has **not** published any 2027 PCB/MTD specification, TP1 2027 or PCB schedule. Its employer page still lists only 2026 documents (page last updated 29 Jul 2026).

### Inferences
- The rate bands (ITA Sch. 1 Part I) and the s.46(1)(a) self relief can only change through an Act of Parliament amending the ITA 1967. Exemption orders cannot do it. So none of the YA2027 individual changes is law until a Finance Act is gazetted.
- Precedent (section 3 below) puts first reading about 5½–6 weeks after the budget speech. For a 9 Oct 2026 budget that means about 16–20 Nov 2026, so the bill not yet being tabled on 6 Nov is consistent with the normal timetable.

## 2. Which ITA 1967 provisions would change, and from which YA?

### Takeaway
The main individual changes, effective **YA2027** (income earned 1 Jan–31 Dec 2027), are:
- Self relief under s.46(1)(a) rises from RM9,000 to RM12,000.
- The resident rate schedule (Sch. 1 Part I, applied via s.6(1)(a)) is restructured. The 70,001–100,000 band drops from 19% to 18%. A new 100,001–150,000 band is taxed at 24% instead of 25%. The 28% band now tops out at RM1m instead of RM2m, and 30% applies above RM1m.
- Several s.46 reliefs get a wider scope, mostly with the caps unchanged.

### Cited Findings
**Rates (proposed, YA2027)**
- "Individual tax relief will be increased from RM9,000 to RM12,000 per annum." Resident rates are reduced to 18% for RM70,001–100,000 and 24% for RM100,001–150,000 — [EY Tax Snapshots](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-my/insights/tax/malaysia-budget-2027/documents/ey-tax-snapshots-of-budget-2027.pdf)
- "The individual income tax rate for resident individuals with chargeable income of above RM1 million will be increased to 30%. Currently, the rate for the chargeable income tax band of between RM1 million and RM2 million is 28%, with the 30% rate applying for chargeable income exceeding RM2 million." — [EY Tax Snapshots](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-my/insights/tax/malaysia-budget-2027/documents/ey-tax-snapshots-of-budget-2027.pdf)
- Skrine's proposed schedule, effective YA2027:

  | Band (RM) | Rate (%) |
  |---|---|
  | 0–5,000 | 0 |
  | 5,001–20,000 | 1 |
  | 20,001–35,000 | 3 |
  | 35,001–50,000 | 6 |
  | 50,001–70,000 | 11 |
  | 70,001–100,000 | 18 (was 19) |
  | 100,001–150,000 | 24 (was 25) |
  | 150,001–400,000 | 25 |
  | 400,001–600,000 | 26 |
  | 600,001–1,000,000 | 28 |
  | >1,000,000 | 30 |

  Skrine also states that the individual relief increase from RM9,000 to RM12,000 is "effective from YA 2027" — [Skrine, Oct 2026](https://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2026/tax-measures-under-the-2027-malaysian-budget)
- The current (YA2026) schedule, as LHDN's 2026 PCB specification Table 1 shows it, has these bands: 5,001–20,000 at 1%, 20,001–35,000 at 3%, 35,001–50,000 at 6%, 50,001–70,000 at 11%, 70,001–100,000 at 19%, 100,001–400,000 at 25%, 400,001–600,000 at 26%, 600,001–2,000,000 at 28%, and above 2,000,000 at 30% — [LHDN, Amendment to Specification for MTD Calculations Using Computerized Calculation for 2026 (PDF), "Updated: 01 January 2026"](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/spesifikasi-kaedah-pengiraan-berkomputer-pcb-2026.pdf)
- Deloitte's Sim Kwang Gek estimates savings of about RM840 at RM100,000 chargeable income and about RM1,520 at RM150,000. She says RM1m–2m earners "could face an additional tax liability of up to RM20,000" — [The Edge, 9 Oct 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821289)
- The individual relief rise is described as the first revision "since 2010" — [Malay Mail, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/09/budget-2027-individual-tax-relief-rises-to-rm12000-income-tax-percentage-lowered/238401)
- The YA2026 return (filed in 2027) is unchanged: RM9,000 relief and the current bands — [Panda HRMS, 15 Oct 2026](https://pandahrms.com/budget-2027-individual-income-tax-relief-changes-ya2027/)

**Other s.46 reliefs (proposed)**
- EY lists these expansions and says "The relief amounts remain unchanged":
  - Medical treatment relief (up to RM10,000) expanded to postnatal care services.
  - Parents and grandparents care relief (up to RM8,000) expanded to all care-related expenses.
  - Sports equipment to include sports shoes.
  - Education and skills training relief expanded to all fields of study and children's tuition fees.
  - Lifestyle relief expanded to AI subscriptions and pet vaccination and adoption.

  SOCSO relief is expanded to cover mandatory LINDUNG KENDIRI contributions, plus an additional relief of up to RM150 for voluntary LINDUNG 24 JAM and LINDUNG KENDIRI contributions — [EY Tax Snapshots](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-my/insights/tax/malaysia-budget-2027/documents/ey-tax-snapshots-of-budget-2027.pdf)
- Postnatal care relief is reported as "up to RM3,000" within a renamed "Health and Well-being" category capped at RM10,000 — [Malay Mail, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/10/having-a-baby-budget-2027-proposes-up-to-rm3000-tax-relief-for-postnatal-care/238498) (via search summary; the article was not fetched directly) **[lower confidence]**
- A search-result summary of Panda HRMS mentioned education and training reliefs being merged under a single RM7,000 limit. This conflicts with EY's "relief amounts remain unchanged" and was not confirmed on fetch — [Panda HRMS](https://pandahrms.com/budget-2027-individual-income-tax-relief-changes-ya2027/) **[unverified, conflicting]**
- Equity crowdfunding and angel investor incentives are extended to investments made 1 Jan 2027–31 Dec 2030 — [Skrine](https://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2026/tax-measures-under-the-2027-malaysian-budget)
- Non-MSME employers can deduct salaries only if paid through financial institutions or prescribed cashless channels, from YA2028 — [The Edge search summary / Budget 2027 coverage](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821179) **[lower confidence; not fetched]**

**How such amendments are drafted (precedent)**
- Finance Act 2025 (Act 874) amended ITA s.46 (s.6 of the Act) and Schedule 1 (s.17). Its commencement clause ties each amendment to a YA, e.g. "have effect for the year of assessment 2026 and subsequent years of assessment" — [Act 874, Finance Act 2025 (PDF, lom.agc.gov.my)](https://lom.agc.gov.my/ilims/upload/portal/akta/outputaktap/3276537_BI/Act%20874%20-%20FINANCE%20ACT%202025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Finance Bill will almost certainly:
  - amend s.46(1)(a) (RM9,000 to RM12,000) and Part I of Schedule 1 (new band table);
  - amend several s.46(1) paragraphs for the relief expansions (medical/health (c)/(ca), parents care, lifestyle, education, SOCSO s.46(1)(n) or similar);
  - carry a commencement clause "for the year of assessment 2027 and subsequent years of assessment."

  This follows the Act 874 pattern; exact paragraph references are unverified until the bill is published.
- My own arithmetic on the proposed bands gives cumulative tax of RM9,100 at RM100,000 (vs RM9,400 now) and RM21,100 at RM150,000 (vs RM21,900). It reproduces Deloitte's figures exactly:
  - RM100k: RM300 rate saving + RM3,000 × 18% = RM840.
  - RM150k: RM800 + RM3,000 × 24% = RM1,520.

  This cross-checks that the reported band table is internally consistent.

### Gaps
- The Finance Bill text is not available, so the exact ITA paragraph numbers, the final relief caps and any merged relief limits are unconfirmed.
- The RM7,000 combined education limit claim could not be verified.

## 3. When were past Finance Acts gazetted relative to their budgets, and what does this imply for Budget 2027?

### Takeaway
The last two cycles followed a very consistent pattern:
- Budget speech in mid-October.
- Finance Bill first reading about 4½–6 weeks later (18–19 Nov).
- Dewan Rakyat passage in early December.
- Dewan Negara mid-December.
- Royal assent 24–27 Dec.
- Gazettement on **31 December**.
- Changes effective 1 Jan or the new YA.

For Budget 2027 this points to a Finance Act gazetted around 31 Dec 2026 (inferred), just in time for YA2027.

### Inferences
- If the pattern holds, the Budget 2027 Finance Bill would see:
  - first reading about mid/late Nov 2026, likely after or alongside the end of the Supply Bill committee stage (26 Nov);
  - Dewan Rakyat passage in early December;
  - Dewan Negara by mid/late December;
  - royal assent in late December and gazettement around 31 Dec 2026.

  On that timeline the law would be in place before 1 Jan 2027, so YA2027 changes would be enacted before YA2027 begins. This is a projection, not a confirmed schedule.
- There is little slack. If the Dewan Rakyat sitting runs late, or the Dewan Negara does not sit before the year-end break, gazettement could slip into early 2027. The changes would still apply to the full YA2027, since they are framed by YA, not by date of operation. PCB would then catch up through the year-end MTD formula, which recomputes on a cumulative basis.

## 5. Do LHDN or payroll vendors say when employers should apply the new rates (January 2027 payroll vs after gazettement)?

### Takeaway
LHDN has said nothing that I could find. Payroll vendors and HR blogs uniformly advise waiting until LHDN publishes the 2027 MTD schedule/specification rather than changing PCB on their own. None commits to January 2027 payroll.

### Cited Findings
- Panda HRMS (15 Oct 2026): higher reliefs and lower rates "can nudge monthly PCB (MTD) down once LHDN updates the schedule". It gives no specific month — [Panda HRMS](https://pandahrms.com/budget-2027-individual-income-tax-relief-changes-ya2027/)
- A search summary of several Malaysian HR and payroll guides (DataOn, Omni HR, Links International) reports advice that employers should not adjust employee PCB until LHDN publishes the updated 2027 MTD schedules — [DataOn](https://dataon.com/en-my/blog/malaysia-payroll-guide/); [Omni HR](https://www.omnihr.co/blog/malaysia-tax); [Links International](https://linksinternational.com/blog/malaysia-budget-2027-employers-hr-updates/) **[search summary only; individual pages not fetched; lower confidence]**
- LHDN's position on how PCB is set: MTD is determined using the computerised calculation method or the e-PCB schedule via e-CP39 — [HASiL, Pembayaran PCB](https://www.hasil.gov.my/en/majikan/pembayaran-pcb/) (search snippet). LHDN-approved payroll software must comply with the published specification — [HASiL approved software list](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/List-of-Software-Provider-Payroll-System-approved-as-at-25-May-2026.pdf)
- I found no statements from the other main payroll vendors specific to Budget 2027 PCB timing in this research.

### Inferences
- In practice, employers and approved software will apply the YA2027 figures from the first payroll after LHDN releases the 2027 specification and updates e-PCB. If precedent holds (spec dated 1 Jan), that is January 2027 payroll. If LHDN releases later, PCB under-reductions early in the year are self-correcting: the MTD formula computes a cumulative annual liability (it uses the accumulated remuneration and MTD paid to date), so later months absorb the difference and final liability is settled on the YA2027 return (filed 2028).
- A financial calculator should treat YA2027 figures as "proposed" until gazettement, and the 2027 PCB figures as "unpublished" until LHDN releases the spec.

### Gaps
- I found no LHDN statement or FAQ on Budget 2027 PCB effective timing.
- I did not find announcements from the main payroll vendors for 2027.

## 6. Is there any risk or report that the proposals could change before enactment?

### Takeaway
I found no reports of planned changes or opposition to the individual rate and relief proposals. Precedent suggests a low risk of material change: Finance Act 2025 had "no material changes" from its bill. But until the bill is published, the exact drafting (paragraphs, caps, merged limits) is uncertain. Sources disagree on some relief details.

### Cited Findings
- KPMG: Finance Act 2025 was gazetted with "no material changes" from the Finance Bill 2025 — [KPMG Malaysia, Jan 2026](https://kpmg.com/my/en/home/insights/2026/01/gazette-of-the-finance-and-tax-acts-2025.html)
- The Supply Bill 2027 passed the policy stage by voice vote (majority support) on 29 Oct 2026 — [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/823823)
- Tax-firm commentary is broadly positive, with no calls for reversal: Deloitte, KPMG, EY and CTIM quoted — [The Edge, 9 Oct 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821289)
- The Malay Mail article does not state the YA or legislative status — [Malay Mail, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/09/budget-2027-individual-tax-relief-rises-to-rm12000-income-tax-percentage-lowered/238401)
- Discrepancies between sources:
  - **Top band:** Deloitte frames it as the RM1m–2m band rising from 28% to 30%. One search summary described it as "setting" 30% for >RM1m. Both describe the same proposal, and EY confirms it is an increase for RM1m–2m earners — [EY](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-my/insights/tax/malaysia-budget-2027/documents/ey-tax-snapshots-of-budget-2027.pdf)
  - **Education relief:** EY says the amounts are unchanged, while one vendor summary says a merged RM7,000 limit (unverified, see section 2).
  - **Postnatal relief:** reported as a "RM3,000 sub-limit within RM10,000" (Malay Mail) vs a simple expansion of medical relief (EY).

  These need the bill text to resolve.

### Inferences
- The government holds a working majority (the Supply Bill passed by voice vote), and the recent Finance Bills passed with no material change. So the headline numbers (RM12,000; 18%/24%; 30% above RM1m) are likely to survive into law. Detailed relief drafting is the most likely area for differences from press summaries.
