Access date for all URLs: 2026-11-06 unless noted. "MY" = Malaysian practice; "foreign" = US/UK/AU/OECD practice. "[LOWER CONFIDENCE]" = single-source, secondary-only, or search-snippet-only claims. Several primary pages (finhealthnetwork.org toolkit text, kwsp.gov.my, FCA Handbook HTML/PDF, moneysmart.gov.au) blocked or returned no readable text to the fetch tool (403 or empty), so some primary claims rest on search-result extracts of those pages. ## 1. Established financial health frameworks and scores (indicators, weighting, tier labels)

### Takeaway
The two most-used composite scores are the US Financial Health Network's FinHealth Score (8 behavioural/outcome indicators across Spend, Save, Borrow, Plan; equal-weight average 0-100; three tiers Healthy 80-100 / Coping 40-79 / Vulnerable 0-39) and the CFPB Financial Well-Being Scale (10 subjective statements; 0-100; six descriptive bands, explicitly with no single good/bad cut-off). Malaysia has no consumer-facing composite score from a regulator; BNM measures capability via the MYFLIC index in its triennial survey, and the most concrete MY public benchmark a calculator can borrow is EPF's Retirement Income Adequacy (RIA) savings tiers.

### Cited Findings
**FinHealth Score (foreign, US; Financial Health Network)**
- 8 survey questions aligned to four pillars, Spend, Save, Borrow, and Plan (now labelled "Plan and Protect"). Output is one overall FinHealth Score plus four subscores (Save, Spend, Borrow, Plan and Protect). Households are classified Financially Healthy, Coping or Vulnerable. — [FHN FinHealth Score Toolkit page](https://finhealthnetwork.org/tools/financial-health-score/finhealth-score-methodology/); [FHN Score page](https://finhealthnetwork.org/score/)
- Scoring: people who answer all 8 questions get a 0-100 score by averaging their responses to the 8 questions. That makes it an equal-weight average, with two questions feeding each pillar subscore. Tier cut-offs are 0-39 Vulnerable, 40-79 Coping, 80-100 Healthy. "Financially Healthy" means healthy outcomes reported across all eight indicators. — [FHN Financial Health Pulse 2025 U.S. Trends Report (PDF)](https://finhealthnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Financial-Health-Pulse-2025-U.S.-Trends-Report.pdf); [FinHealth Score Toolkit 2021 (PDF)](https://finhealthnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/FinHealthScoreToolkit-2021.pdf) (cut-offs and averaging confirmed via search extracts of these PDFs; PDF text was not machine-readable to the fetch tool)
- A March 2026 FHN brief refreshed "several survey questions to improve accuracy, clarity, and interpretability". It frames the work as refining the Score, not replacing it. Results can be benchmarked against a nationally representative US sample by age, income, location and other demographics. — [FHN, From Insight to Impact: The Next Phase of Financial Health Measurement (2026)](https://finhealthnetwork.org/research/from-insight-to-impact-the-next-phase-of-financial-health-measurement/); [PDF](https://finhealthnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/From-Insight-to-Impact_-The-Next-Phase-of-Financial-Health-Measurement.pdf) [LOWER CONFIDENCE on whether the 80/40 cut-offs changed in 2026; not confirmed]
- Free for nonprofit research, advocacy and internal business use; other uses require a licence. — [FHN Toolkit page](https://finhealthnetwork.org/tools/financial-health-score/finhealth-score-methodology/)

**CFPB Financial Well-Being Scale (foreign, US)**
- 10 questions in two parts. Six "how well does this describe you" items use a 5-point scale (Completely ... Not at all). Four "how often" items use a 5-point scale (Always ... Never). Age group (18-61 / 62+) and administration mode (self-read vs read aloud) change the scoring. The tool measures "how well your current money situation is providing you with financial security and freedom of choice, today and for the future". — [CFPB Financial well-being questionnaire](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/financial-well-being/)
- Score is 0-100; higher means more measured well-being, "but there is not a specific cut-off for a 'good' or 'bad' score". — [CFPB Quick Guide to the Financial Well-Being Scale (PDF)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201701_cfpb_FinancialWell-Being_Quick-Guide.pdf) (via search extract)
- Current descriptive bands: Very Low 0-29, Low 30-37, Medium Low 38-49, Medium High 50-57, High 58-67, Very High 68-100. Experiences vary widely within each range. — [CFPB, Quick guide to financial well-being score ranges](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tool/financial-well-being-resources/measure-and-score/)
- Earlier CFPB materials used different bands (≤40, 41-50, 51-60, 61-70), set at "key break points in Financial Well-Being Scores in the U.S. population". The bands are therefore empirical and population-derived, and they have been revised. — [CFPB webinar deck, 2019 (PDF)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/20190131_cfpb_webinar_financial-well-being-toolkit.pdf) [LOWER CONFIDENCE: search extract]

**OECD/INFE (foreign/international)**
- The 2023 International Survey of Adult Financial Literacy covered 39 countries/economies (20 OECD members) using the 2022 OECD/INFE Toolkit. Financial literacy scores run 0-100 (knowledge + behaviour + attitudes), and a minimum target score of 70 is used as a benchmark. Scoring details are in Annex C. — [OECD/INFE 2023 survey](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-infe-2023-international-survey-of-adult-financial-literacy_56003a32-en.html); [PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/12/oecd-infe-2023-international-survey-of-adult-financial-literacy_8ce94e2c/56003a32-en.pdf)
- The survey also produces financial resilience and financial well-being measures. One extract says 4 items relate to objective financial resilience, rescaled to 0-50. Higher literacy is associated with higher well-being and resilience after controlling for socio-economic characteristics. — same sources [LOWER CONFIDENCE on the 4-item/0-50 detail: fragmentary extract]
- A new OECD/INFE Toolkit (January 2026) covers the next data-collection round, including financial literacy, inclusion and well-being. — [OECD/INFE Toolkit 2026 (PDF)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/01/oecd-infe-toolkit-for-measuring-financial-literacy-inclusion-and-well-being-2026_6e8d9566/92f2d439-en.pdf)

**Malaysia (MY)**
- BNM's Financial Capability and Inclusion (FCI) Demand Side Survey runs every three years. It combines financial knowledge, behaviour and attitude into the Malaysia Financial Literacy and Capability (MYFLIC) Index. This is an OECD/INFE-style population measure, not a personal score. — [AFI case study on BNM financial literacy (PDF, 2024)](https://www.afi-global.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Promoting-Financial-Literacy-Case-Study-of-Bank-Negara-Malaysia.pdf) (via search extract)
- FCI 2024: 61% of Malaysians struggled to raise RM1,000 for an emergency, up from 47% in 2021. One commentary also reports that only 37% could sustain living expenses for more than three months if income stopped. — [Malay Mail op-ed by Mohamad Fazli Sabri and Amirah Shazana Magli, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/what-you-think/2026/09/29/are-malaysian-households-truly-financially-resilient-mohamad-fazli-sabri-and-amirah-shazana-magli/236923); [Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2513957) [LOWER CONFIDENCE on the 37% figure: commentary, survey wording unconfirmed]
- The National Strategy for Financial Literacy 2026-2030 (Financial Education Network) targets cutting the share unable to raise RM1,000 for an emergency from 61% to no more than 45% by 2030. "RM1,000 emergency" is therefore an official MY resilience indicator. — [FEN National Strategy 2026-2030 (PDF)](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN_NS2_ENG_Interactive_FA_LowRes.pdf); [BNM Governor speech, National Financial Literacy Symposium 2025 (BIS)](https://bis.org/review/r251002n.htm)
- EPF RIA framework: three savings targets at age 60. Basic RM390,000 (60% of Adequate), Adequate RM650,000 (240 × Belanjawanku 2024/2025 "Adequate Retirement Income", rounded down to the nearest RM10,000), Enhanced RM1.3 million (2 × Adequate). It was launched December 2024 and took effect January 2026. It replaces the single RM240,000-by-55 Basic Savings benchmark used 2008-2025, and is to be reviewed every three years from 2029. — [The Star, 12 Dec 2024](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2024/12/12/epf-launches-three-tier-savings-framework-for-different-retirement-lifestyles); [Malay Mail, 12 Dec 2024](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/12/12/epf-rolls-out-new-retirement-guide-and-savings-plan-to-help-malaysians-prepare-for-the-future/159611); [MAICSA technical announcement (PDF)](https://www.maicsa.org.my/media/9802/technical_announcements_241213_1_1.pdf)
- Belanjawanku's Adequate Retirement Income figure is reportedly RM2,690/month for a single senior in the Klang Valley (× 240 = RM645,600, rounded to RM650,000). — [calculatormalaysia.com (third party)](https://calculatormalaysia.com/tax/epf-retirement-calculator-malaysia/) [LOWER CONFIDENCE: third-party; arithmetic is consistent with "240 × ... rounded" in primary-derived coverage]
- Phase-in of Basic Savings: sources conflict. One says it starts at RM290,000 in 2026 and rises to RM390,000 by 2028. Another says it rises "RM50,000 annually over three years", reaching RM390,000 by 2028. Others present RM390,000 as the target with no phase-in. — [imoney.my](https://www.imoney.my/articles/epf-retirement-savings-level-increase); [ricebowl.my](https://www.ricebowl.my/career-advice/blog/en/32560/epf-retirement-income-adequacy-framework); [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/budgeting-saving/epf-introduces-belanjawanku-2024-2025-and-three-tier-ria-framework-to-boost-financial-literacy-and-retirement-planning.html) [CONFLICT; verify on kwsp.gov.my, which returned 403]
- Around 36% of active formal members met the then-existing basic level as of October 2024. — [search summary of EPF launch coverage, e.g. The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2024/12/12/epf-sets-basic-retirement-savings-goal-at-rm390000) [LOWER CONFIDENCE]. In March 2026 an EPF official was reported saying 60% of members could reach the basic savings level by 2030. — [FMT, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2026/03/05/60-of-epf-members-can-reach-basic-savings-level-by-2030-says-amir-hamzah) (headline only)
- Under the 2026 rules, early withdrawals are allowed only if the remaining balance can still provide a minimum retirement income. — [RinggitPlus (secondary)](https://ringgitplus.com/en/guides/find-out-how-much-you-need-to-retire-with-this-calculator/) [LOWER CONFIDENCE]

### Inferences
- The FinHealth Score is the closest model for a "good/bad shape" verdict. It is equal-weight, has only 3 coarse tiers, and is built from self-reported outcomes ("spending less than income", "manageable debt"), not exact ratios. A ratio-based MY tool would be a different instrument and should not borrow the FinHealth tier names as though it were validated.
- The CFPB's explicit "no cut-off for good or bad", together with its revised, population-derived bands, is strong precedent for showing descriptive bands rather than pass/fail.
- For MY, the only official, published, personal-level benchmarks found are the EPF RIA tiers (retirement) and the FEN "RM1,000 emergency" indicator (resilience). Both are MY-native and citable, which suits an education-only app.
- Because RIA amounts are fixed nominal figures for "at age 60" and are reviewed every three years, a projection compared against them must state the as-of year. It must also note that the targets will likely rise (see Section 4).

### Gaps
- The exact wording of the eight FinHealth indicators and their response-point values was not readable in this session (the PDF was binary to the fetch tool). From background knowledge, not verified here, they are roughly: spend less than income; pay bills on time; sufficient liquid savings; confidence in long-term savings; manageable debt; prime credit score; adequate insurance; plan ahead. Each answer maps to 0/25/50/75/100. Verify from the Toolkit PDF before quoting.
- UK Money and Pensions Service (MaPS) / Money Advice Service measures: no results came back. Not covered here are the UK Strategy for Financial Wellbeing 2020-2030 national goals (e.g. the "savings cushion" target) and the MAS Financial Capability Survey indicators. They need a dedicated lookup at maps.org.uk.
- AKPK: no AKPK financial health score or self-assessment tool was found. Unknown whether one exists.
- CFPB US average score and band distribution were not confirmed (the oft-cited "54" is unverified).

## 2. Single ratios: which are authoritative vs rules of thumb

### Takeaway
Few single ratios have an official consumer-facing threshold. In Malaysia the citable ones are EPF RIA (retirement savings at 60), the FEN/BNM "can you raise RM1,000" emergency measure, and DSR, which banks use for lending. The commonly quoted DSR ceiling of about 60% is a lending practice, not a regulator-set household health line. Savings rate targets, "3-6 months emergency fund", net-worth multiples and age-based retirement multiples are mostly industry rules of thumb.

### Cited Findings
- **Debt service ratio (MY):** PIDM's FinLit page says banks generally accept a DSR below 60% across all debt commitments, counting non-bank debts too. — [PIDM, What is Debt Service Ratio](https://www.pidm.gov.my/finlit/manage-your-financial-commitments/what-is-debt-service-ratio). Commercial guides say BNM provides general guidance but each bank sets its own ceiling, usually around 60%, sometimes 70-80%+ for higher earners. They also say DSR is typically measured on net income, with only 50-80% of variable income counted. — [RinggitPlus DSR guide](https://ringgitplus.com/en/guides/debt-service-ratio-dsr-explained/); [CIMB, Understanding DSR](https://www.cimb.com.my/en/personal/life-goals/credit-and-debt/how-much-debt-is-too-much-understanding-dsr-debt-service-ratio-in-malaysia.html); [StashAway](https://www.stashaway.my/r/debt-service-ratio-dsr-malaysia) [LOWER CONFIDENCE on any "BNM 60%" rule: no primary BNM document found]
- A BNM Financial Stability Review figure, cited by a commercial site, puts the median DSR on outstanding household loans at 33% at end-December 2025. — [bumiexpo.com (secondary)](https://www.bumiexpo.com/blog/debt-service-ratio-malaysia/) [LOWER CONFIDENCE; verify in BNM FSR 2H2025]
- **Emergency savings (MY):** the official indicator is the ability to raise RM1,000 for an emergency, tracked by the FCI survey with a 2030 target of no more than 45% unable. — [FEN National Strategy 2026-2030](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN_NS2_ENG_Interactive_FA_LowRes.pdf). No official BNM "3-6 months" benchmark was found in this session; commentary uses "more than three months of expenses" as a descriptive survey measure. — [Malay Mail op-ed, 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/what-you-think/2026/09/29/are-malaysian-households-truly-financially-resilient-mohamad-fazli-sabri-and-amirah-shazana-magli/236923)
- **Retirement savings vs age (MY):** EPF RIA tiers at 60 (RM390k / RM650k / RM1.3m), which are official — see Section 1. The previous single benchmark was RM240,000 at 55. — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2024/12/12/epf-launches-three-tier-savings-framework-for-different-retirement-lifestyles)
- **Emergency savings (foreign):** the FinHealth "sufficient liquid savings" indicator is expressed in months of living expenses as a self-reported outcome, not as a fixed regulatory ratio. — [FHN Toolkit page](https://finhealthnetwork.org/tools/financial-health-score/finhealth-score-methodology/) (pillar structure only confirmed)

### Inferences
- Authoritative and MY-specific: EPF RIA tiers (official EPF), the RM1,000 emergency indicator (official FEN/BNM strategy target) and DSR. DSR is authoritative only as "what lenders typically use", not as a health line.
- Rules of thumb (would need to be labelled as such or avoided): "save 10-20% of income", "3-6 months of expenses", "debt-to-income below 36%/43%" (US mortgage-underwriting origin), "net worth = age × income / 10", and Fidelity-style "1× salary by 30" multiples. None was verified against a primary source in this session; all are US industry heuristics.
- A defensible design is to show each ratio as a neutral number. Only where an official MY reference exists would the app add a "compared with [named source, year]" line, for example "Banks commonly approve financing when DSR is below about 60% (PIDM)", and otherwise state that no official benchmark exists.

### Gaps
- No primary BNM document setting a DSR threshold for households (vs. lenders' internal policy) was retrieved. The BNM Policy Document on Responsible Financing would be the place to check.
- No primary source was retrieved for US DTI thresholds (CFPB Ability-to-Repay 43%) or for savings-rate recommendations. These are from background knowledge only and are not cited here.
- AKPK's counselling criteria (e.g. the DSR level at which the Debt Management Programme applies) were not found.

## 3. Presenting an indicator without it becoming advice or shaming

### Takeaway
Regulators who publish scores avoid pass/fail. The CFPB says there is no good/bad cut-off and warns that showing a score can make some people anxious. Evidence on the "ostrich effect" shows people look at their finances less when things are worse, so a harsh verdict risks disengagement. Best practice from these sources: descriptive bands, the benchmark and its source shown inline, variation within bands acknowledged, and a check on whether to show a score at all.

### Cited Findings
- CFPB: the score ranges give "a sense of whether a person has high, medium, or low financial well-being", and experiences vary widely within each range. Practitioners should consider "whether, and how, to share the score". Seeing a score right away may make some people anxious or upset and may motivate others, so they should listen and move toward a supportive conversation. — [CFPB, Quick guide to financial well-being score ranges](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tool/financial-well-being-resources/measure-and-score/)
- CFPB: "there is not a specific cut-off for a 'good' or 'bad' score". — [CFPB Quick Guide (PDF)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201701_cfpb_FinancialWell-Being_Quick-Guide.pdf)
- CFPB consumer tool framing: "See how your score compares to other U.S. adults from our national survey" and "Review steps you can take" — a peer comparison plus educational next steps, not a verdict. — [CFPB questionnaire](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/financial-well-being/)
- FHN offers peer benchmarks by age, income, location and demographics, so people can be compared with similar households. — [FHN Toolkit page](https://finhealthnetwork.org/tools/financial-health-score/finhealth-score-methodology/)
- The ostrich effect: Olafsson & Pagel find people check their accounts less when their finances look worse. Attention drops as balances turn negative and falls further as overdraft debt grows. — [NBER w23945, The Ostrich in Us](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23945); [published version (RESTAT)](https://www.arnaolafsson.com/uploads/2/3/7/5/23754531/checking_restat_final.pdf)
- VoxEU summary of the same research: selective attention may intensify problems such as debt traps. One extra login per month was associated with about a 17% reduction in consumer debt over 24 months. This is observational, not causal proof. — [VoxEU/CEPR, The ostrich in us](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/ostrich-us-selective-attention-personal-finances) [LOWER CONFIDENCE on the causal reading]

### Inferences
- Wording: prefer descriptive, CFPB-style labels ("lower / middle / higher than the benchmark") and "compared with [benchmark], [source], [year]" over judgments ("bad", "failing"). If tiers are used, FinHealth's "Vulnerable / Coping / Healthy" exist as a precedent, but they label people. Labelling the measure instead ("your emergency buffer: below the EPF/FEN reference") is less personal.
- Colour/status: red/green pass-fail implies a verdict. The sources support neutral or sequential palettes with text labels (also needed for colour-blind users), plus a visible benchmark marker and its source link.
- Ranges over single verdicts: CFPB's "experiences vary widely within each range" supports showing a band and the number together, not a single traffic light.
- Ostrich-effect evidence suggests leading with what the person can see and explore, not a headline grade, and avoiding alarming copy for people who are already struggling, who are the users most at risk of disengaging.
- Not-advice line (inference, to be checked against the parallel MY regulatory research): describing where a number sits against a named public benchmark is information. Saying what the user should do with their money moves toward advice.

### Gaps
- No experimental study was found in this session testing how people react to receiving a composite financial-health score (e.g. framing, peer comparison, or colour effects on behaviour). FHN/CFPB user testing, if published, was not located. This needs a targeted literature search (e.g. "social comparison savings field experiment", the Beshears et al. peer-information retirement-savings study, which is known from background knowledge but not verified here).
- No UK FCA/MaPS or Australian guidance on visual presentation of health scores was retrieved.

## 4. Wealth and retirement projections: methods, regulator assumptions, showing uncertainty

### Takeaway
Regulated projections in the UK and Australia are deterministic compound-growth calculations with prescribed or capped rates. They are shown in today's money using a prescribed inflation rate of 2.5%, and in the UK as multiple scenarios (2/5/8% nominal). Neither the UK nor Australia defaults to Monte Carlo for consumer illustrations, but both now favour range or variability messaging. EPF's own calculator assumptions could not be retrieved, and third-party MY calculators use a constant user-chosen dividend rate with no inflation adjustment, which overstates real outcomes against RIA's nominal targets.

### Cited Findings
**UK (foreign)**
- FCA COBS 13 Annex 2 projection rates: 2% / 5% / 8% (lower / intermediate / higher) nominal for personal and stakeholder pensions, tax-exempt wrappers and investment-linked annuities; 1.5% / 4.5% / 7.5% for other products. Three rates must be used for pre-sale personal pension projections. Rates were cut from 5/7/9% in 2012 after CP12/10 and PS12/17. — [FCA Handbook COBS 13 Annex 2](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/13/Annex2.html); [FCA CP12/10 (PDF)](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consultation/cp12-10.pdf); [FCA PS12/17 (PDF)](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps12-17.pdf) (rates confirmed via search extracts; the Handbook pages returned no readable text to the fetch tool; the annex shows last updated 19 Nov 2025)
- The FCA's 2017 review of prescribed rates (prepared for the FCA) re-examined the intermediate rate and the long-term assumptions of 2.5% price inflation and 4% earnings inflation. — [FCA, Rates of return for FCA prescribed projections (Sept 2017)](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research/rates-return-fca-prescribed-projections.pdf)
- UK Statutory Money Purchase Illustrations (annual pension statements) are shown in today's money using a prescribed 2.5% p.a. inflation rate. Under the FRC actuarial standard AS TM1 (v5.x), each fund's accumulation rate is set by a prescribed method based on the fund's historical volatility group and reviewed annually. — [Railpen, A guide for members on SMPI (Apr 2026, PDF)](https://cdn3.railpen.com/mp-sitefinity-prod/docs/librariesprovider2/read-as-you-needs/a-guide-to-statutory-money-purchase-illustration-(smpi)apr26.pdf?sfvrsn=4420eba1_3); [FCA CP12/10](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consultation/cp12-10.pdf)
- Critique of the volatility-group method: industry body TISA argued that "the higher the group, the greater potential there is for the associated growth rate to be consistently inaccurate", and that the fallback for funds with short histories was vague. — [TISA response to FRC AS TM1 consultation, 2022 (PDF)](https://www.tisa.uk.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/FRC-AS-TM1-final.pdf)

**Australia (foreign)**
- ASIC superannuation forecasts relief (ASIC Instrument 2022/603; guidance now in RG 276): the default nominal wage inflation was lowered from 4% to 3.7% p.a. from 1 January 2025, to align with the 2023 Intergenerational Report. Wage inflation applies in the accumulation phase and CPI of 2.5% p.a. in retirement. Forecasts must be expressed in present values (today's dollars) using the defaults unless the user enters another rate. — [ASIC, updates superannuation forecasts relief instrument](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/asic-updates-superannuation-forecasts-relief-instrument); [ASIC CP351 (PDF)](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/ru2ddxs0/cp351_published_18_november_2021.pdf); [CP351 Attachment 2 (PDF)](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/awzh252f/attachment-2-to-cp351-published-18-november-2021.pdf)
- Moneysmart calculators: inflation defaults (wage inflation before retirement, CPI after) are editable in Advanced Settings. The superannuation calculator's returns are net of tax and investment fees, while the account-based pension calculator's are gross of tax and net of fees. Dollar-based admin fees rise with inflation. The calculators warn that actual returns "will vary significantly from year to year and could be negative in some years". — [Moneysmart account-based pension calculator](https://moneysmart.gov.au/retirement-income-sources/account-based-pension-calculator); [Moneysmart superannuation calculator](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/superannuation-calculator) (via search extracts; moneysmart.gov.au returned 403 to direct fetch)
- Critique: the Moneysmart planner applies a constant return each year with no option to stress-test volatility. A third party said its 7.5% assumed return plus low fees could overstate balances (dated commentary). Super Consumers Australia's 2024 review compared default assumptions (inflation, wage growth, returns, fees) across retirement calculators. — [SuperGuide on Moneysmart Retirement Planner](https://www.superguide.com.au/retirement-planning/moneysmart-retirement-planner); [Citro guide](https://www.joincitro.com.au/news/calculating-retirement---a-citro-guide); [Super Consumers Australia calculators report 2024 (PDF)](https://superconsumers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Calculators-report-final-1.pdf) [LOWER CONFIDENCE: secondary; SCA report not opened]

**Malaysia (MY)**
- EPF RIA targets are nominal amounts derived as 240 months × a current (2024/2025) monthly Belanjawanku retirement income. The method implies no inflation adjustment over the 20-year drawdown and no investment return in drawdown. — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2024/12/12/epf-launches-three-tier-savings-framework-for-different-retirement-lifestyles); [calculatormalaysia.com](https://calculatormalaysia.com/tax/epf-retirement-calculator-malaysia/) [LOWER CONFIDENCE on the "not inflation-adjusted" reading: inferred from the stated formula]
- Illustrative payouts in launch coverage: RM390,000 supports about RM1,625/month in the first year of retirement; RM650,000 about RM2,708/month (i.e. ÷240). — [search summary of EPF launch coverage](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2024/12/12/epf-launches-three-tier-savings-framework-for-different-retirement-lifestyles) [LOWER CONFIDENCE]
- Third-party KWSP calculators (not EPF) assume a constant user-selected dividend rate (one uses 5.50% "your assumption, not a forecast"), fixed annual salary increases, no withdrawals, and a constant Akaun split. They note that EPF credits dividends on the daily balance and that contributions follow the Third Schedule wage table, so real statements differ. — [dividen.my KWSP calculator](https://dividen.my/kwsp); [kwsp.hamradio.my](https://kwsp.hamradio.my/); [calculatormalaysia.com](https://calculatormalaysia.com/tax/epf-retirement-calculator-malaysia/) [LOWER CONFIDENCE: non-official]
- Commentary (Feb 2026) argues that for most members EPF savings may no longer be enough, and that targets assume consistent contributions and a stable career. — [FMT, 3 Feb 2026](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2026/02/03/for-most-epf-savings-may-no-longer-be-enough) (headline/snippet only)

### Inferences
- A standard deterministic method is FV = balance × (1+r)^n + Σ contributions × (1+r)^(n−t), with r applied monthly or annually (to match EPF's annual dividend crediting). Salary growth g drives contributions, and the result is deflated by (1+i)^n to show today's money. This matches the UK SMPI and ASIC approach (nominal growth, then deflate by a prescribed inflation rate).
- A UK-style multiple-rate presentation (e.g. three nominal dividend scenarios) is the most regulator-precedented way to show uncertainty without Monte Carlo. It is also more honest for EPF, whose historical dividends have varied year to year. Historic EPF dividend data was not retrieved in this session.
- Comparing a projected nominal balance at 60 with RIA's nominal RM390k/650k (2024/25 prices) mixes price bases. Either deflate the projection to today's ringgit and compare with RIA as published, or inflate RIA. The app should state which one it does.
- Required caveats with precedent: "figures are estimates, not guaranteed", "returns vary and can be negative" (Moneysmart), "shown in today's money using X% inflation" (SMPI/ASIC), and the as-of year of benchmarks and rates.
- Monte Carlo: no regulator source in this session mandates it for consumer tools. Its main critique precedent (constant returns hide volatility; SuperGuide/SCA) argues for at least a range.

### Gaps
- EPF's own retirement tools (i-Akaun / KWSP website calculators) and their default dividend, inflation and salary-growth assumptions could not be retrieved (kwsp.gov.my returned 403; no official documentation surfaced). This is a key gap for MY alignment.
- Historic EPF dividend rates and any official long-run return assumption used by EPF were not retrieved.
- Exact current COBS 13 Annex 2 text was not read directly: the rule capping projection rates where a product's investment potential is lower, the required "not guaranteed" wording, and the treatment of charges. Treat the 2/5/8% figures as confirmed by multiple extracts but verify the wording.
- An older TISA document said COBS assumed 2% inflation. This conflicts with the 2.5% in newer sources, so it is probably outdated, but it is unresolved.
- Moneysmart's current default return and fee figures were not confirmed (site blocked).

## 5. Accessibility and clarity of numbers for people new to finance

### Takeaway
Little direct guidance was retrieved in this session. The strongest transferable practices from the regulator sources above are expressing results in today's money, giving a plain-language meaning alongside each number, showing the benchmark source, and offering peer comparison rather than abstract scores.

### Cited Findings
- UK SMPI and ASIC forecasts translate future balances into present-value or today's-money terms specifically to make them meaningful to consumers. — [Railpen SMPI guide (PDF)](https://cdn3.railpen.com/mp-sitefinity-prod/docs/librariesprovider2/read-as-you-needs/a-guide-to-statutory-money-purchase-illustration-(smpi)apr26.pdf?sfvrsn=4420eba1_3); [ASIC news item](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/asic-updates-superannuation-forecasts-relief-instrument)
- EPF framing converts lump-sum targets into a monthly income (RM390k ≈ RM1,625/month), a more intuitive unit for most people. — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2024/12/12/epf-launches-three-tier-savings-framework-for-different-retirement-lifestyles) [LOWER CONFIDENCE]
- The FEN national strategy uses a concrete, everyday measure ("raise RM1,000 for an emergency") instead of an abstract ratio. — [FEN National Strategy 2026-2030](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN_NS2_ENG_Interactive_FA_LowRes.pdf)
- The CFPB tool's administration mode (self-read vs read aloud) changes scoring, which acknowledges that how a measure is delivered affects responses. — [CFPB questionnaire](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/financial-well-being/)

### Inferences
- For newcomers: show lump sums as monthly-income equivalents and in today's ringgit, and use one concrete reference per indicator (RM1,000 emergency, RIA tier). Put the formula and assumptions behind a "how this is calculated" disclosure. Bilingual BM/EN wording would matter in MY. Not researched here.
- Avoid stacking many scores. FinHealth uses only 3 tiers and 4 pillars, which shows that a small number of categories is considered communicable.

### Gaps
- No numeracy or plain-language research (e.g. FCA consumer testing of projections, MaPS research on pension statement comprehension, studies on percentages vs natural frequencies) was retrieved in this session. Needs a targeted follow-up.
- No MY-specific usability or financial-literacy guidance on number presentation (BNM/FEN/AKPK) was found.
