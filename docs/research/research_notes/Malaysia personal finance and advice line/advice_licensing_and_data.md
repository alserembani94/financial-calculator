# Malaysia: where financial education ends and regulated advice begins (licensing, advertising and personal data)

Scope: a free, neutral, education-only personal-finance web app for Malaysian users, with no bank integration and no product sales. Current as of 6 November 2026. All sources were accessed 6 November 2026 unless stated otherwise.

**This is research, not legal advice.** The notes summarise primary texts where I could read them (CMSA Schedules 2 and 3, the SC Guidance Note on Provision of Investment Advice) and law-firm or press commentary where I could not. Interpretations marked "(uncertain)" or "Inference" are my reading, not settled law. Section 7 lists what a Malaysian lawyer should confirm.

## 1. CMSA 2007: "investment advice", "financial planning", the CMS Licence and CMS Representative's Licence, and the exclusions

### Takeaway
"Investment advice" is regulated only when it is carried on **as a business** (or analyses or reports are issued **as part of a business**), and it concerns **securities or derivatives**. "Financial planning" is defined more broadly: it means analysing **another person's** financial circumstances and giving them **a plan**, "whether or not a fee is charged". That second definition is the one a personalised finance app is most at risk of meeting. The statutory exclusions (Schedule 3) are narrow, and a free web app does not obviously fit any of them. In particular, the newspaper and "information service" exclusions apply only to publications sold **for value** to subscribers or purchasers.

### Cited Findings
- **Schedule 2, Part 1 (regulated activities)** lists: (1) dealing in securities; (2) dealing in derivatives; (3) fund management; (4) advising on corporate finance; (5) **investment advice**; (6) **financial planning**; (7) **dealing in private retirement schemes** (inserted by P.U.(A) 336/2011); (8) clearing for securities or derivatives. — [SC, CMSA Schedule 2 & 3 (PDF)](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=090e71ab-fa25-4c23-8bc0-cfcaee0da913)
- **Schedule 2, Part 2, para 5:** "'Investment advice' means carrying on a business of advising others concerning securities or derivatives or as part of a business, issues or promulgates analyses or reports concerning securities or derivatives." [Am. P.U.(A)336/2011] — [SC, CMSA Schedule 2](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=090e71ab-fa25-4c23-8bc0-cfcaee0da913)
- **Schedule 2, Part 2, para 6:** "'Financial planning' means analysing the financial circumstances of another person and providing a plan to meet that other person's financial needs and objectives, including any investment plan in securities, whether or not a fee is charged in relation thereto." — [SC, CMSA Schedule 2](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=090e71ab-fa25-4c23-8bc0-cfcaee0da913)
- **Schedule 2, Part 2, para 7:** "Dealing in private retirement schemes" covers "making or offering to make with any person, or inducing or attempting to induce any person, to enter into" an agreement to acquire or dispose of an interest in, or contribute to, a PRS. — [SC, CMSA Schedule 2](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=090e71ab-fa25-4c23-8bc0-cfcaee0da913)
- **Schedule 2, Part 2, para 1(b):** "Dealing in securities" includes "inducing or attempting to induce any person to enter into" an agreement to acquire securities. — [SC, CMSA Schedule 2](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=090e71ab-fa25-4c23-8bc0-cfcaee0da913)
- **Schedule 3 (specified persons, s 58(2)), relevant exclusions:**
  - Para 2 and para 3: an advocate and solicitor, or an accountant in practice, whose financial planning is "solely incidental to the practice of his profession".
  - Para 5: a newspaper proprietor holding a Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 permit. The exclusion applies only where (a) the newspaper is distributed only to subscribers and purchasers "for value"; (b) the advice is given only through that newspaper; (c) the proprietor receives "no commission or other consideration for issuing or promulgating the analyses or reports"; and (d) the advice is "solely as incidental to" the newspaper business.
  - Para 6: the same four conditions for a person providing an "information service" distributed "only to subscribers for, and purchasers of, the information service for value".
  - Source: [SC, CMSA Schedule 3](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=090e71ab-fa25-4c23-8bc0-cfcaee0da913)
- **Section 58 and the penalty:** Schedule 2 is referenced from subsections 2(1) and 58(1). The SC states that "any person undertaking a regulated activity without a licence by the SC is a punishable offence under the CMSA which carries a maximum fine of RM 10 million or a maximum imprisonment term of 10 years or both" (Guidance Note para 3.8). A 2022 conviction under s 58(1) for unlicensed fund management describes the same maximum. — [SC Guidance Note SC-GN/1-2020 (R2-2024)](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0); [Business Today, 14 Nov 2022](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2022/11/14/former-company-director-jailed-for-unlicensed-fund-management-activities/)
- **Other offences that apply to anyone, licensed or not:** market manipulation; false or misleading statements likely to induce buying or selling; and recklessly making "any statement, promise or forecast that is misleading, false or deceptive to induce or attempt to induce another person to deal in securities". The penalty is up to 10 years' imprisonment and a minimum fine of RM1 million (Guidance Note para 3.5). — [SC Guidance Note (R2-2024)](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **CMS Licence vs CMS Representative's Licence:** the Licence is held by the firm, the Representative's Licence by an individual acting for it. The Guidance Note para 3.3 states that a CMSRL holder may run trading seminars only when "representing his Principal (CMSL)", and only where this is incidental to his role. — [SC Guidance Note (R2-2024)](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Financial planners need a CMSRL in financial planning (CMSRL-FP):** training and career sources state that a financial planner must hold a valid CMSRL-FP, citing the SC Licensing Handbook 2022. These are secondary sources. — [howtofinancemoney (secondary)](https://howtofinancemoney.com/cfp-certified-financial-planner); [myscholar (secondary)](https://myscholar.my/seo/cara-jadi-perancang-kewangan-malaysia)

### Inferences
- **The two definitions are triggered differently.** "Investment advice" requires (a) securities or derivatives and (b) a business. "Financial planning" requires (a) analysing **another person's** circumstances and (b) giving **a plan to meet their needs and objectives**. A fee is expressly irrelevant to the definition. Section 58 still requires the activity to be **carried on as a business**: the paragraph 3.8 penalty is framed as undertaking a "regulated activity" without a licence, and s 58(1) is generally summarised as "carry on a business in any regulated activity". (Uncertain, because I could not read the s 58 text itself; see Gaps.) Whether a free, non-commercial app is "carrying on a business" is a key question for a lawyer.
- **The highest-risk design** is a feature that takes a user's own salary, debts and goals and outputs "your plan" or "you should do X". That matches the wording of the financial-planning definition closely, even if no product is named and no fee is charged.
- **Calculators** are generic tools. A calculator applies a formula to whatever numbers the user enters and gives no plan or recommendation, so it fits less naturally within either definition. (Inference.)
- **The newspaper and information-service exclusions are unlikely to cover a free web app.** Both require distribution "for value" to subscribers or purchasers. A free app is not sold for value, so it falls outside them, and they should not be relied on. (Inference; uncertain.)

### Gaps
- I could not read the official text of CMSA ss 58, 59 (representatives) and 76 (registered persons, Schedule 4) from agc.gov.my or sc.com.my in this session. The s 58 penalty above comes from the SC Guidance Note and a news report on a s 58(1) conviction, not from the Act. The phrases "carry on a business" and "hold himself out" in s 58(1) need checking against the Act.
- The Schedule 2 and 3 PDF on sc.com.my carries 2018 metadata. I could not confirm whether these Schedules were amended after 2018. The amendment notes stop at P.U.(A) 133/2017.
- The current SC Licensing Handbook was not read, so the competency modules for a CMSRL in financial planning are unverified.

## 2. FSA 2013 and IFSA 2013: financial advisory business, financial adviser approval, insurance and takaful advice, and the financial-planner bodies (FPAM, MFPC)

### Takeaway
Under the FSA, "financial advisory business" is **limited to insurance**. It covers analysing a person's financial-planning needs relating to an insurance product, recommending one, and arranging it. It needs **Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) approval under s 11**, and carrying it on without approval carries penalties of up to RM50 million or 10 years' imprisonment. IFSA has a parallel regime for takaful. Malaysia has no single "registered financial planner" licence. A practitioner who gives full financial planning typically holds both an **SC Capital Markets Services Representative's Licence in financial planning (CMSRL-FP)** and a **BNM Financial Adviser's Representative** status, and qualifies through **FPAM** (CFP, IFP) or **MFPC** (RFP, Shariah RFP) credentials. Those credentials are prerequisites for the licences, not licences themselves.

### Cited Findings
- **FSA s 2(1), "financial advisory business"** covers five activities, all tied to insurance: analysing "the financial planning needs of a person relating to an insurance product"; recommending an insurance product; sourcing one from a licensed insurer; arranging an insurance contract; and any other activity prescribed under s 3. — [Low & Partners, FSA 2013 Part 1](https://www.lowpartners.com/financial-services-act-2013-part-1/)
- **Approval, s 11.** An "approved financial adviser" is a person approved under s 11. BNM assesses applications against Schedule 5, Part 1. — [Low & Partners](https://www.lowpartners.com/financial-services-act-2013-part-1/)
- **Exemptions, s 8(2).** Persons in Division 2 of Part 1 of Schedule 1 are exempt from the approval requirement. I did not see that list. — [Low & Partners](https://www.lowpartners.com/financial-services-act-2013-part-1/)
- **Penalties.** Carrying on an authorised business without approval (s 8(3)) carries up to 10 years' imprisonment, a fine of up to RM50 million, or both. — [Low & Partners](https://www.lowpartners.com/financial-services-act-2013-part-1/)
- **IFSA 2013 (Act 759)** has a parallel definition of Islamic financial advisory business and of "approved Islamic financial adviser", tied to takaful products. Under FSA s 15(1)(c), an approved financial adviser may carry on Islamic financial advisory business with BNM's written approval, and it is then subject to the IFSA. — [IFSA 2013 PDF (investmalaysia.gov.my)](https://www.investmalaysia.gov.my/media/drcdqgbk/islamic-financial-services-act-2013.pdf); [Low & Partners](https://www.lowpartners.com/financial-services-act-2013-part-1/)
- **Dual licensing.** Secondary sources describe two tracks for financial planning. The SC route is the CMSRL-FP. The BNM route is the Financial Adviser's Representative (FAR), or the Islamic FAR (iFAR). The qualifying credentials are:
  - **Registered Financial Planner (RFP)** and **Shariah RFP**, conferred by the **Malaysian Financial Planning Council (MFPC)**;
  - **Certified Financial Planner (CFP)** and **Islamic Financial Planner (IFP)**, conferred by the **Financial Planning Association of Malaysia (FPAM)**. The IFP is run jointly with IBFIM.
  - Sources: [FPAM IFP page](https://fpam.org.my/membership/be-a-ifp-professional/); [IBFIM IFP](https://info.ibfimonline.com/ifp/); [howtofinancemoney (secondary)](https://howtofinancemoney.com/cfp-certified-financial-planner)
- **FPAM's role.** It is a professional and certification body whose credentials feed licence eligibility. It is not a regulator. One comparison site says its paid planners are "licensed by the Securities Commission of Malaysia and members of the Financial Planning Association of Malaysia (FPAM)". — [RinggitPlus Financial Planner launch](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/financial-planning/ringgitplus-launches-first-fully-digital-one-to-one-financial-planning-service-ringgitplus-financial-planner.html)
- **MFPC's public stance.** MFPC acts as an industry voice on unlicensed advice. In January 2026 its president welcomed the SC's revised advertising guidelines as "an important first step" and said: "Unlicensed advice erodes trust, but professionalism restores it." — [The Star, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/01/17/calls-to-regulatefinfluencers)

### Inferences
- **Insurance features.** For an app, the FSA risk arises if it analyses a user's protection needs and **recommends** an insurance or takaful product, or a specific coverage amount tied to a product. Explaining generically what term life, medical cards or takaful are should fall outside the definition, because the definition is framed around analysing a person's needs and recommending or arranging a product. (Inference; uncertain.)
- **A "how much insurance do I need" calculator** that outputs a personalised coverage figure sits closest to limb (a) of the definition ("analysing the financial planning needs of a person relating to an insurance product"). It needs careful framing, or removal.
- **Whether a business is needed.** The FSA definition uses "business". As with the CMSA, a free, non-commercial app may arguably not be carrying on a business, but this needs legal confirmation.

### Gaps
- The FSA Schedule 1, Part 1, Division 2 exemption list was not read. The FSA PDF text could not be extracted because of its font encoding.
- I did not find an official BNM page explaining what "advice on insurance" means for generic online tools or comparison sites. I also did not find whether BNM's Policy Document on Management of Customer Information, or its Fair Treatment of Financial Consumers rules, apply to non-licensees. They likely do not.
- The term "registered financial planner" in the brief appears to refer to the CMSRL-FP plus BNM FA combination, or to the MFPC "RFP" credential. I found no separate statutory "registered financial planner" status. Also unconfirmed: whether the SC still recognises "registered persons" under CMSA Schedule 4 who may conduct financial planning.

## 3. SC and BNM guidance: finfluencers, social-media investment content, robo-advice, and enforcement against unlicensed advice

### Takeaway
The SC's **Guidance Note on Provision of Investment Advice** (SC-GN/1-2020, first issued 30 Dec 2020, revised 4 Apr 2022 and 18 Jul 2024) is the controlling text. It turns on two questions:
1. **Is it advice?** That is, a "recommendation or opinion" likely to induce someone to buy, sell or hold a "particular class, sector, or instrument".
2. **Is it a business?** That is, done with regularity, or with pay-for-advice, a paid subscription, or any "expectation of benefits or gratification", including view income or referral rewards.

Sharing "factual information" or "educating" is "not likely" to need a licence, and **a disclaimer alone does not cure advice**. The revised **Advertising Guidelines** (R1-2025, in force 1 Nov 2025) treat independent finfluencers as "advertisers". Digital or robo advice is still licensed investment advice.

### Cited Findings
- **Para 2.2, what counts as advice.** "Any communication involving providing recommendations or opinions which are likely to induce a person to take any action or position (e.g. buy, sell or hold) regarding a particular class, sector, or instrument in relation to securities or derivatives, is likely to be considered as 'advising others concerning securities or derivatives'." — [SC Guidance Note (R2-2024)](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Para 2.2, the "business" factors.** The SC is more likely to treat the activity as a business where it is "undertaken in a structured manner with regularity", or where any of the following is present:
  - (a) "pay-for-advice arrangements";
  - (b) "a fee-based subscription to a channel or group, including on social media";
  - (c) "expectation of benefits or gratification, direct or indirectly".
  - The list is "not intended to be exhaustive". Source: [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Para 2.2, disclaimers.** "Having a disclaimer that states that you are not providing an investment advice, in itself, does not relieve you from the requirement of holding a licence." — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Para 3.2, training and seminars.** Seminars on "general stock trading techniques" are generally not licensable, even for a fee. A licence is needed if content "amounts to a recommendation or inducement to take action or position ... regarding a particular class, sector, or instrument". — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Para 3.4, disclosure when commenting.** Anyone commenting on securities on radio, TV or "any other manner of communication" is expected to disclose (i) whether they are SC-licensed and (ii) any interest they hold in the securities. Disclosure does not absolve them from the licensing assessment. — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Para 3.7, "five funds I think will generate positive return".** "Generally, talking or sharing factual information about an investment product or towards educating the followers (as opposed to advising) would not likely require a licence from the SC." A recommendation or opinion that may induce action is likely to be advice. "Expecting benefits or gratification ... such as income generated by views on the social media platform or referral rewards" counts towards "carrying on a business". — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Para 3.8 and 3.9.** Promoting a trading platform may amount to investment advice or **dealing in securities**. A "genuine user experience" is generally fine, but "affiliate marketing" counts towards carrying on a business. — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Para 3.6, conduct standard for licensees.** This applies to licensees but is a useful benchmark. Examples of false or misleading representations include "stating that an investment is capital guaranteed when it is not, or promising an investment that will give high return with minimum risk of loss". — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Para 1.3, status of the note.** The note "is not intended to be a legal advice provided by the SC and does not prevent the SC from taking an action should the need arise". — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Revision history.** The note was first issued on 30 Dec 2020, revised as R1-2022 on 4 Apr 2022 and as R2-2024 on 18 Jul 2024. Some press and secondary sources refer to an "April 2025 update" to the Guidance Note. I found no R3-2025 version, so that reference appears to conflate the note with the March 2025 Advertising Guidelines revision. — [SC Guidance Note list of revisions](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0); [SC Guidance Notes page](https://www.sc.com.my/regulation/guidance-notes-and-guiding-principles)
- **Advertising Guidelines R1-2025.** Issued 27 Mar 2025 and effective 1 Nov 2025. Their key provisions are:
  - Finfluencers who advertise capital market products and services "on their own accord", without being engaged as marketing agents, "would be regarded as advertisers".
  - Advertisers are accountable for their marketing agents' compliance.
  - Persons not authorised by the SC are prohibited from advertising services in Malaysia.
  - Sources: [SC media release](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/media-release/sc-issues-revised-guidelines-on-advertising-for-capital-market-products-and-related-services); [Rahmat Lim & Partners](https://www.rahmatlim.com/publication/articles/30455/securities-commission-issues-revised-guidelines-on-advertising-for-capital-market-products-and-related-services)
- **Enforcement figures.** Secondary press reports say the SC recorded 77 unlicensed-investment-advice cases in 2024, up from 22 in 2023 (a 250% increase). This comes from search snippets only; I did not read the SC primary source. — [The Star, 14 Apr 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/04/14/money-talks---but-should-you-listen-when-finfluencers-offer-investment-tips); [Wahed (secondary)](https://www.wahed.com/mme/what-just-happened-with-malaysian-finfluencers)
- **September 2026.** Berita Harian reported that the SC monitors finfluencers and takes action against unlicensed advice. Its example of advice is telling followers to buy a particular stock at a set price and time. The report also said finfluencers may not promote unlicensed or unregistered platforms. This is from a search snippet; the page returned 403. — [Berita Harian, Sept 2026](https://www.bharian.com.my/bisnes/lain-lain/2026/09/1614145/sc-pantau-finfluencer-tindakan-terhadap-pemberi-nasihat-tanpa-lesen)
- **January 2026 reaction.** Fomca reported sharply rising complaints about finfluencers. The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) was named as a first line of defence under ss 211 and 233 of the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998. — [The Star, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/01/17/calls-to-regulatefinfluencers)
- **Robo-advice and digital investment management.** The SC introduced the Digital Investment Management framework in May 2017 for automated discretionary portfolio management by licensed fund managers. On 8 Dec 2022 it issued a Technical Note on digital investment advice, meaning advice delivered through "automated, algorithm-based tools". Providers need an SC licence, and may apply to have some capital and competency requirements waived. — [SC media release, May 2017](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/media-release/sc-introduces-regulatory-framework-to-facilitate-digital-investment-management-services); [SC media release, 8 Dec 2022](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/media-release/sc-issues-new-guidelines-and-technical-note-to-enhance-the-quality-and-diversity-of-investment-advisory); [Conventus Law on Technical Note 1/2022](https://conventuslaw.com/report/malaysia-legal-updates-financial-services-december-2022/)

### Inferences
- **The Guidance Note's framing maps directly onto app features.** Generic, factual, educational content is "not likely" licensable. Anything that produces a **recommendation or opinion about a particular class, sector or instrument** is the danger zone. "Class" and "sector" are broad, so "put more into equities" or "shift to bonds" could arguably be a recommendation about a class. (Inference; uncertain.)
- **A free app with no ads, affiliates or referrals has fewer "business" indicators.** The "expectation of benefits" test is broad, though. Ad revenue, affiliate links, sponsorship, lead generation or a later paid tier would all add weight.
- **The SC treats "automated, algorithm-based" advice as investment advice.** An app that algorithmically maps a user's profile to an asset allocation or a product is not protected by being software.
- **Finfluencer advertising rules.** These would matter mainly if the app's social or marketing presence promoted specific capital market products or platforms. A neutral education app that promotes no product is outside the core of those rules. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- I did not read the full text of the Advertising Guidelines R1-2025. Not verified: the definition of "advertisement", whether educational material is excluded, and the content rules on projections, past performance and risk warnings.
- I found no BNM-specific finfluencer guidance on insurance or takaful and banking products.
- I found no named 2025–2026 court prosecution of a finfluencer for unlicensed advice. Specific SC enforcement-action pages were not reviewed.
- The full text of Technical Note 1/2022 (the definition of "digital investment advice") was not read.

## 4. How each feature type maps onto the regimes: calculators, health scores, DTI against bank thresholds, projections, named products, and AI chat

### Takeaway
Risk rises as a feature moves along this sequence:
1. **Generic arithmetic.** Lowest risk.
2. **A descriptive comparison of the user's own numbers against published third-party benchmarks.**
3. **A personalised verdict or score.**
4. **A plan or "what you should do".** Close to the "financial planning" wording.
5. **Naming or ranking specific products or instruments, or classes of them.** Close to "investment advice", FSA "financial advisory business", or "dealing" by inducement.

AI chat that answers personal questions can drift into stages 4 and 5 without any change to the design. It is the hardest feature to keep on the education side.

### Cited Findings
- **General calculators.** Trading-technique seminars are "not likely" licensable unless content recommends a "particular class, sector, or instrument". Factual or educational sharing is "not likely" licensable. — [SC Guidance Note paras 3.2, 3.7](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Comparable calculator wording.** A comparison site's loan-eligibility calculator says its results are indicative, do not constitute a loan offer or approval, and that final credit decisions rest with the lender. This comes from a search snippet. — [iMoney eligibility calculator](https://www.imoney.my/imoney-pre-screening?src=hp_sb)
- **"Financial planning" covers analysis plus a plan, whether or not a fee is charged.** — [CMSA Sch 2 para 6](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=090e71ab-fa25-4c23-8bc0-cfcaee0da913)
- **Debt-service ratio (DSR) benchmarks:**
  - BNM's Responsible Financing policy (in effect since 2012) underpins lenders' DSR assessments.
  - The median DSR was 34% on outstanding loans and 41% on new loans in 2024.
  - BNM's 2022 Financial Stability Review reported that two-thirds of new loans had DSRs below 60%.
  - AKPK has been cited as recommending a DSR of 30–40%, in a 2013 opinion column.
  - The Perbadanan Insurans Deposit Malaysia (PIDM) has been cited, by a comparison site, as treating a 20–40% DSR as "safe".
  - I could not confirm that 60% is a formal regulatory cap. Sources: [The Vibes (MOF reply)](https://www.thevibes.com/articles/news/105330/mof-household-debt-at-rm1.63-trillion-by-end-of-2024-still-well-below-financial-assets); [Malay Mail, 29 Mar 2023](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2023/03/29/bnm-risk-of-default-low-but-25pc-of-household-borrowers-have-high-service-ratio/62125); [RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/bnm-25-household-borrowers-have-high-debt-service-ratio-but-default-risk-is-low.html); [Malay Mail 2013 column](https://www.malaymail.com/news/what-you-think/2013/07/04/household-debt-not-worrying-eh-sobry-ahmad/490469)
- **Projections.** It is an offence for anyone to "recklessly make any statement, promise or forecast that is misleading, false or deceptive to induce or attempt to induce another person to deal in securities". Promising "high return with minimum risk" is given as an example of a misleading representation. — [SC Guidance Note paras 3.5, 3.6](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Products in scope:**
  - Units in unit trusts and stocks are securities.
  - PRS has its own regulated activity, "dealing in private retirement schemes", which includes "inducing or attempting to induce" contributions.
  - Insurance and takaful advice falls under the FSA or IFSA.
  - Sources: [CMSA Sch 2](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=090e71ab-fa25-4c23-8bc0-cfcaee0da913); [Low & Partners on FSA](https://www.lowpartners.com/financial-services-act-2013-part-1/)
- **Algorithmic or automated advice is still investment advice requiring a licence.** — [SC media release, 8 Dec 2022](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/media-release/sc-issues-new-guidelines-and-technical-note-to-enhance-the-quality-and-diversity-of-investment-advisory)
- **A comparable Malaysian health-check product exists.** A comparison site launched a free financial health check and separately sells one-to-one planning through SC-licensed, FPAM-member planners. This shows a commercial site separating its free tool from licensed advice. I could not read the article's details because the page returned 404. — [RinggitPlus search result](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/ringgitplus/ringgitplus-launches-free-financial-health-check-tool-for-all-malaysians.html); [RinggitPlus Financial Planner](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/financial-planning/ringgitplus-launches-first-fully-digital-one-to-one-financial-planning-service-ringgitplus-financial-planner.html)

### Inferences
Feature by feature (all inferences; the risk levels are my judgement):
- **General calculators** (loan instalment, compound interest, EPF or tax arithmetic, DSR arithmetic). **Low risk.** They are generic formulas run on numbers the user enters, with no recommendation about any class, sector or instrument and no "plan". Keep outputs numeric and assumption-labelled.
- **A "financial health" score or "good shape / bad shape" indicator.** **Low to medium risk.** A score is an evaluation of "the financial circumstances of another person", which is the first half of the financial-planning definition. It becomes riskier when paired with the second half, a "plan to meet ... needs and objectives". Safer framing:
  - show each component metric against **a cited published benchmark**, such as "AKPK suggests 30–40%" or "BNM data: median new-loan DSR 41%";
  - describe the result in neutral terms, such as "above / within / below the cited range";
  - avoid a single verdict like "you are in bad shape" with prescriptive next steps.
  
  Avoid any composite "grade" that implies a recommendation about products.
- **DTI or DSR compared with bank lending thresholds.** **Low to medium risk** for licensing. The main risk is **misleading-representation risk**, because there is no single statutory DSR cap and banks' internal thresholds vary. Present thresholds as "commonly cited" ranges, with sources and dates. Never say "you will or won't get approved". Do not imply a link to any lender, and do not suggest which lender to approach (that would steer towards products).
- **Wealth or retirement projections with assumed returns.** **Medium risk.** The licensing risk is low if the user chooses the return rate, or several neutral scenarios are shown (for example 2%, 4% and 6%). The risk rises if:
  - the app assigns a return tied to a named product, such as "ASB has paid ~5%, so you'll have RMx"; or
  - it suggests an allocation to reach the target.
  
  Misleading-forecast risk exists under the CMSA forecast offence if a projection induces dealing in securities. Label projections as illustrations, not forecasts. Show the assumptions, the effect of inflation, and that returns are not guaranteed.
- **Naming or comparing specific products** (ASB, unit trusts, PRS funds, stocks, insurance or takaful plans). **High risk.**
  - Purely factual descriptions are "not likely" licensable (para 3.7). Examples: "ASB is a unit trust fund managed by ASNB", "PRS contributions qualify for tax relief up to RMx", or a link to the official fact sheet.
  - **Ranking, "best", or "suitable for you" outputs are likely recommendations or opinions.** So is matching a user to a product, or a side-by-side comparison with a winner.
  - For PRS, "inducing or attempting to induce" contributions is itself a licensed **dealing** activity.
  - For insurance, recommending a product after analysing needs is FSA financial advisory business.
- **AI chat answering personal questions.** **High risk.** A model given the user's numbers and asked "should I put my bonus into ASB or pay off my car loan?" will naturally produce a personalised recommendation, which is close to both definitions. The SC treats automated, algorithm-based advice as investment advice. Guardrails are essential:
  - refuse or redirect product-specific and "should I buy, sell or hold" questions;
  - answer conceptually (how the trade-off works, with a worked example the user can adjust);
  - avoid naming instruments as recommendations;
  - log and test prompts that try to extract recommendations;
  - consider leaving AI chat out of v1.

### Gaps
- I found no SC or BNM guidance that addresses **calculators, scores or health indicators** specifically. The mapping above is inferred from the definitions and the Guidance Note.
- I found no Malaysian enforcement case involving a free calculator or education app.
- The BNM Responsible Financing policy document was not read, so the exact DSR methodology and any thresholds are unverified.

## 5. PDPA 2010 and the 2024 amendments: obligations if users enter salary, debts and investments, and server storage vs on-device storage

### Takeaway
The Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (PDPA), as amended by the Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024, applies to personal data processed "in respect of commercial transactions". The 2024 amendments came into force in phases in 2025:
- "data user" was renamed "data controller";
- data processors became directly subject to the Security Principle;
- breach notification was introduced (72 hours to the Commissioner, 7 days to individuals);
- DPO appointment became mandatory above thresholds (both from 1 Jun 2025);
- cross-border transfer rules changed (1 Apr 2025);
- a data-portability right was added, with guidelines expected in 2026.

Financial information is **not** "sensitive personal data" under the Act. It **is** counted in the DPO threshold guideline, however (more than 10,000 data subjects). Keeping all data **on the user's device**, never transmitted to the operator, is the strongest way to minimise PDPA exposure. Whether the operator then "processes" any personal data at all is a question for a lawyer.

### Cited Findings
- **Amendment Act and commencement.** The amendment (Act A1727) received royal assent on 9 Oct 2024. On 24 Dec 2024 the Minister of Digital set phased commencement dates. Sources disagree on the first phase: some say 1 Jan 2025 for administrative changes, others 1 Apr 2025 for definitions and processor duties. — [Data Protection Report (Norton Rose Fulbright)](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2025/01/new-horizons-in-data-protection-malaysias-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024/) (search snippet; page returned 403); [Baker McKenzie alert](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/-/media/files/insight/publications/alerts/08/malaysia-public-consultation-on-the-personal-data-protection-regulations/malaysia-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024-to-come-into-force--ba.pdf?sc_lang=en&rev=8447c31f6f2c4689a91136a64e860979&hash=8F2471D0FD4605717B390D3A750F630B)
- **Scope.** Personal data is limited to information "in respect of commercial transactions". It excludes deceased persons and credit-reporting data. — [DLA Piper Data Protection Laws of the World: Malaysia](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=MY)
- **Sensitive personal data:**
  - It covers health, political opinions, religious beliefs, offences, **biometric data** (added by the 2024 Amendment Act) and any category the Minister prescribes.
  - As of 10 Feb 2026, no further categories had been prescribed.
  - Financial information is not listed.
  - Sources: [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=MY); [Linklaters, Data Protected: Malaysia](https://linklaters.com/insights/data-protected/data-protected---malaysia)
- **DPO (in force 1 Jun 2025).** A DPO is required where processing involves:
  - more than 20,000 data subjects;
  - "sensitive personal data, including financial information" of more than 10,000 data subjects; or
  - regular and systematic monitoring.
  
  The DPO must be proficient in Malay and English, and resident in Malaysia or easily contactable. The Commissioner must be notified within 21 days, and contact details must be published. — [DLA Piper Privacy Matters, 4 Mar 2025](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/); [DLA Piper DPLOTW](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=MY)
- **Breach notification (s 12B, in force 1 Jun 2025):**
  - The Commissioner must be notified "as soon as practicable" and within 72 hours. Late notices need written reasons with evidence.
  - Affected data subjects must be told without unnecessary delay and within 7 days of notifying the Commissioner.
  - "Significant harm" includes financial loss, an effect on credit records, misuse for illegal purposes, compromise of sensitive data, identity fraud risk, and (for the Commissioner only) more than 1,000 affected data subjects.
  - A breach register must be kept for at least 2 years.
  - Sources: [DLA Piper Privacy Matters](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/); [Edwin Lee & Partners](https://lpplaw.my/insights/e-articles/data-breach-malaysia/)
- **Data processors.** Processors are now directly bound by the Security Principle. A secondary source reports penalties of up to RM1,000,000 and/or 3 years' imprisonment for breaching the principles. — [DLA Piper DPLOTW](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=MY); [Data Protection Report (snippet)](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2025/01/new-horizons-in-data-protection-malaysias-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024/)
- **Data portability.** The 2024 Amendment Act introduced the right, reported as s 43A, subject to "technical feasibility and compatibility of the data format". Guidelines were expected in 2026. — [DLA Piper DPLOTW](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=MY); [Data Protection Report (snippet)](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2025/01/new-horizons-in-data-protection-malaysias-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024/)
- **Cross-border transfers (1 Apr 2025).** Transfers are allowed to jurisdictions with substantially similar laws or equivalent protection, or under listed exceptions. The cross-border transfer guidelines of 29 Apr 2025 require a Transfer Impact Assessment. — [DLA Piper DPLOTW](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=MY)
- **Director liability.** Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for a body corporate's non-compliance, subject to a due-diligence defence. — [DLA Piper DPLOTW](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=MY)
- **Upcoming guidelines.** Guidelines on automated decision-making, DPIAs, data protection by design, and portability were expected in 2026. — [DLA Piper DPLOTW](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=MY)
- **"Processing"** includes "collecting, recording, holding or storing" personal data. — [Linklaters (search snippet)](https://linklaters.com/insights/data-protected/data-protected---malaysia)

### Inferences
- **If data stays only on the user's device** (localStorage or IndexedDB, with no sync, analytics payloads or error logs containing inputs), the operator arguably never "collects, records, holds or stores" users' financial data. PDPA obligations would then attach mainly to whatever the operator does collect, such as IP addresses in server logs, analytics identifiers, and the email addresses of feedback senders. (Inference; needs a lawyer to confirm, including whether a free app's processing is "in respect of commercial transactions".)
- **If inputs are stored server-side** (accounts or sync), the operator is a **data controller** for salary, debt and investment data. It must then follow the seven principles: notice in Malay and English, consent, security, retention and so on. It also needs a breach-response plan for 72-hour and 7-day notification, a processor contract with the hosting provider, and a cross-border transfer assessment if hosting is outside Malaysia. Above 10,000 users' financial data, it must appoint a DPO.
- **Third-party AI APIs.** An AI chat feature that sends the user's numbers to an LLM API engages the processor and cross-border rules even if the core app stores nothing. That is another reason to keep AI out of v1, or to send no personal figures to it.
- **Even with on-device storage,** good practice would include:
  - a short privacy notice in BM and EN explaining that data stays on the device;
  - a "clear my data" control;
  - no third-party scripts that could read inputs.
  
  This is a design recommendation, not a legal requirement I could cite.

### Gaps
- I could not read the official PDPA or Amendment Act text on agc.gov.my or pdp.gov.my. The section numbers 12A, 12B and 43A come from secondary sources.
- I found no PDPC guidance on client-side-only apps or on whether a free, non-monetised service involves "commercial transactions".
- The s 45 exemption (an individual's personal or household affairs) and the extraterritorial reach (s 2: established in Malaysia or using equipment in Malaysia) were not verified from a source in this session.
- The exact current penalty amounts after the amendment were not verified from primary text.

## 6. Consumer protection and advertising rules relevant to calculators and disclaimers

### Takeaway
The Consumer Protection Act 1999 (CPA) prohibits misleading conduct (s 9) and false or misleading representations about goods or services (s 10). These offences arise under s 25. The CPA's application section reportedly **excludes securities and futures contracts**, which are handled under securities law. For a free education app, the practical risks are a misleading calculator output or overclaiming ("accurate", "guaranteed", "bank-approved"). The SC's Advertising Guidelines bite only if the app advertises capital market products or services, which it should not do.

### Cited Findings
- **CPA structure.** Part II covers misleading conduct (s 9), false or misleading representation (s 10) and misleading price indications (s 12). Section 25 makes engaging in these an offence. A secondary source reports fines of up to RM250,000 for some breaches; the penalty is unverified. — [UiTM IR paper](https://ir.uitm.edu.my/28409/1/28409.pdf); [SAYS (secondary)](https://says.com/my/lifestyle/malaysian-consumer-rights); [KPDN Act 599 PDF](https://www.kpdn.gov.my/images/2024/awam/akta/ttpm/Act%20599.pdf) (could not be text-extracted)
- **CPA exclusion.** The Act reportedly excludes "securities" (as defined in the Securities Industry Act 1983) and "futures contracts" (Futures Industry Act 1993). It covers goods and services supplied to consumers in trade, including electronic transactions. The latest amendment listed is Act A1598, effective 1 Oct 2019. This comes from a search snippet; the section numbering is unverified. — [LawPlayer, Act 599](https://lawplayer.com/my/act/act-599)
- **SC Advertising Guidelines R1-2025.** These apply to advertising of capital market products and services, including independent finfluencers, from 1 Nov 2025. — [SC media release](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/media-release/sc-issues-revised-guidelines-on-advertising-for-capital-market-products-and-related-services)
- **Disclaimers do not convert advice into non-advice under the CMSA.** — [SC Guidance Note para 2.2](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **MCMC** can act against misleading online content under CMA 1998 ss 211 and 233. — [The Star, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/01/17/calls-to-regulatefinfluencers)

### Inferences
- **Whether the CPA applies to a free app is uncertain.** It applies to goods or services "supplied ... in trade", and a free, non-commercial app may fall outside "trade". Even so, accuracy and honest labelling reduce risk under the CPA, under the CMSA forecast offence, and in tort (negligent misstatement).
- **Disclaimers help with consumer-protection and misstatement risk.** They set expectations and state the assumptions. They do **not** help with licensing risk if the substance is advice. Design the substance first, then add the disclaimer.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the CPA text (s 2 application, s 3 "services", ss 9, 10, 25 penalties) from an official source in this session. The KPDN PDF could not be text-extracted.
- I found no BNM rule on "financial promotions" by non-licensees. BNM's conduct rules, such as Product Transparency and Disclosure and Fair Treatment of Financial Consumers, generally bind licensed institutions. That is unverified for this use case.
- The Advertising Guidelines' content standards (projections, past performance, risk statements) were not read.

## 7. Practical rules to stay on the education side, how comparable platforms describe themselves, and what a Malaysian lawyer should confirm

### Takeaway
The safest design is a **generic tool plus factual education**:
- no recommendation or opinion on any particular class, sector or instrument;
- no "plan to meet your needs";
- no product naming beyond neutral, factual, sourced descriptions;
- no revenue tied to products (ads, affiliates, referrals);
- benchmarks shown as cited third-party ranges, not verdicts;
- data kept on the device.

Disclaimers are necessary but **not sufficient**, as the SC states expressly.

### Cited Findings
- **The SC's own line.** "Factual information ... or towards educating ... (as opposed to advising)" is "not likely" licensable. Recommendations or opinions inducing buy, sell or hold on a "particular class, sector, or instrument" are likely advice. Benefit expectations (views, referrals, affiliates) indicate a business. A disclaimer alone does not cure advice. — [SC Guidance Note paras 2.2, 3.7, 3.9](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Disclose licensing status and interests** when commenting on securities (para 3.4). — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **No promise of high returns with low risk, and no false capital-guarantee claims** (para 3.6). — [SC Guidance Note](https://www.sc.com.my/api/documentms/download.ashx?id=d22c8909-06e8-40bf-a8da-4fc31b4606c0)
- **Comparable platforms:**
  - One comparison site labels its calculator results as indicative, "not a formal loan offer or approval", and says lenders decide.
  - Another separates its free tools (calculators and a free "Financial Health Check") from paid planning delivered by SC-licensed, FPAM-member planners.
  - Sources: [iMoney calculator](https://www.imoney.my/imoney-pre-screening?src=hp_sb); [RinggitPlus Financial Planner](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/financial-planning/ringgitplus-launches-first-fully-digital-one-to-one-financial-planning-service-ringgitplus-financial-planner.html); [RinggitPlus Financial Health Check (title only; page 404)](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/ringgitplus/ringgitplus-launches-free-financial-health-check-tool-for-all-malaysians.html)
- **The SC encourages users to check licensing** through its Investment Checker. — [The Edge / search summary](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/719566)

### Inferences
These are proposed practical rules, inferred from the sources above.

1. **Wording**
   - Use "illustration", "example", "estimate based on your inputs" and "commonly cited range".
   - Avoid "you should", "we recommend", "best", "suitable for you", "your plan", "buy/sell/hold/switch", "top funds" and "safe investment".
   - Phrase next steps as questions to explore, or as links to official education sources, not instructions.
2. **Indicators**
   - Show each metric (DSR, savings rate, emergency-fund months) separately.
   - Pair each with a **named, dated third-party benchmark**, for example "AKPK has suggested 30–40% DSR" or "BNM FSR: median DSR on new loans 41% (2024)".
   - Describe the result neutrally ("above the cited range").
   - Avoid a composite pass/fail verdict, or tie it to the benchmark source explicitly.
   - Never imply loan approval or rejection.
3. **Projections**
   - Let the user choose the return, or show several neutral scenarios.
   - Show inflation-adjusted values.
   - State that the figures are not forecasts or guarantees.
   - Do not tie the return assumption to a named product's historical dividend.
4. **Products**
   - Use generic categories ("unit trusts", "PRS", "fixed deposits", "takaful") with factual descriptions and links to the official regulator or provider page.
   - No ranking, matching, filtering by user profile, or "winner" comparisons.
   - Factual statutory information (for example "PRS tax relief up to RMx under LHDN rules") is fine.
5. **Money**
   - Take no affiliate links, referral fees, sponsored placements or product ads. These are "benefit or gratification" indicators under para 2.2.
   - If ads are ever added, keep financial products out of them.
6. **AI chat**
   - Omit it in v1, or confine it to explaining concepts, with hard refusals for product-specific or "should I" questions.
   - Send no personal figures to a third-party model.
   - Document the guardrails.
7. **Data**
   - Keep data on the device by default, with no server copies of financial inputs.
   - Publish a bilingual privacy notice.
   - Provide a "delete my data" control.
   - Do not log inputs in analytics or error tracking.
8. **Disclaimer**
   - Include it as a statement of purpose, not as a shield. For example: "General education only. Not investment, financial-planning, insurance or tax advice. We are not licensed by the SC or BNM. We do not sell or promote any financial product. Results depend on your inputs and assumptions. Consult a licensed adviser (check the SC Investment Checker or BNM's list of financial advisers) before making decisions."
   - The SC says a disclaimer alone does not relieve the licensing requirement, so the substance must already comply.
9. **Self-description**
   - Use "free financial education and calculators".
   - Avoid "financial planner", "adviser", "robo-adviser", "wealth manager" and "personalised plan".

**What a Malaysian lawyer should confirm:**
1. Whether a free, non-monetised app is "carrying on a business" under CMSA s 58 and FSA s 8 or s 11. Also whether future monetisation (ads, donations, a paid tier) changes that.
2. Whether a personalised "financial health" score with suggested next steps amounts to "financial planning" under CMSA Sch 2 para 6. In particular, whether "providing a plan" needs a formal plan or covers lighter suggestions.
3. Whether showing allocation scenarios by asset class ("equities vs fixed income") is a recommendation regarding a "particular class" under the Guidance Note.
4. Whether neutral factual pages on ASB, ASM, PRS and Tabung Haji, and on EPF dividend history, are safe. Also whether a projection that uses a historical ASB or EPF dividend rate as the default assumption is safe.
5. Whether an insurance or takaful "coverage needs" calculator engages FSA or IFSA "financial advisory business" limb (a).
6. Whether an AI chat feature, even with guardrails, constitutes "digital investment advice" under SC Technical Note 1/2022.
7. Whether PDPA applies at all if financial inputs never leave the device, and whether a free service involves "commercial transactions". Also what minimal notices are needed for analytics or server logs.
8. Whether the CPA applies to a free service ("in trade"), and what tort or negligent-misstatement exposure the operator has from calculator errors.
9. Whether the SC Advertising Guidelines R1-2025 could catch the app's own social-media content if it mentions specific products, even neutrally.
10. Current CMSA, FSA and PDPA penalty provisions and any amendments after 2024, read from the official Acts on agc.gov.my (LOM).

AKPK, BNM's financial education materials, the SC's InvestSmart and EPF's Belanjawanku were not reviewed in this session. They are good next sources for "how education platforms describe themselves" and for citable benchmarks: Belanjawanku's reference budgets, and AKPK's DSR guidance.
