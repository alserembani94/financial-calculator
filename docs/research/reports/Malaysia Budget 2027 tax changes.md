# Budget 2027 lifts relief, awaits gazette

Budget 2027, tabled on **Friday 9 October 2026**, proposes two headline changes for resident individuals from **YA2027**. Self relief rises from **RM9,000 to RM12,000**, its first increase since YA2010. The rate schedule is also reshaped: the RM70,001–100,000 band drops from **19% to 18%**, a new RM100,001–150,000 band is taxed at **24%** (was 25%), and the 30% top rate now starts above **RM1 million** instead of RM2 million ([MOF Langkah Cukai, Lampiran 1–2](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/ucapan/langkah-cukai.pdf)). As of 6 November 2026 **none of it is law**. No Finance Bill carrying these measures has been reported tabled, and LHDN has published no 2027 PCB/MTD specification. Precedent points to gazettement around **31 December 2026** and an LHDN spec around 1 January 2027. Rebates, the RM35,000 rebate threshold and EPF/SOCSO/EIS rates are untouched. For a single 30-year-old, the illustrative gain in monthly take-home is **RM0 at RM2,000, RM7.50 at RM4,000, RM15.00 at RM5,000, RM65.84 at RM9,000 and RM129.17 at RM15,000**. These figures are proposed, not law. The repo's YA2027 files already match every official figure, so the work after gazettement is mostly flipping status flags, swapping derived values for LHDN-published ones, and confirming the items the budget left unsaid.

## A RM3,000 relief rise and a one-point cut define YA2027

The speech frames the package as relief for the squeezed middle class. Paragraph 29 announces that the relief for the individual and dependent relatives, "kali terakhir disemak pada 2010", rises to RM12,000. It also cuts the 70,001–100,000 and 100,001–150,000 bands by one point each. Paragraph 30 adds that income above RM1 million is "diselaraskan kepada 30 peratus". Paragraph 31 claims **up to RM1,600 more disposable income for about 5 million taxpayers** ([MOF Ucapan Belanjawan 2027](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/ucapan/ub27.pdf)). The tax-measures appendix sets out the full table, effective "Mulai tahun taksiran 2027" ([MOF Langkah Cukai, Lampiran 1](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/ucapan/langkah-cukai.pdf)):

| Chargeable income (RM) | YA2026 (in force) | YA2027 (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 – 5,000 | 0% | 0% |
| 5,001 – 20,000 | 1% | 1% |
| 20,001 – 35,000 | 3% | 3% |
| 35,001 – 50,000 | 6% | 6% |
| 50,001 – 70,000 | 11% | 11% |
| 70,001 – 100,000 | 19% | **18%** |
| 100,001 – 150,000 | 25% | **24%** (new band) |
| 150,001 – 400,000 | 25% | 25% |
| 400,001 – 600,000 | 26% | 26% |
| 600,001 – 1,000,000 | 28% | 28% |
| 1,000,001 – 2,000,000 | 28% | **30%** |
| Above 2,000,000 | 30% | 30% |

EY, Skrine, Deloitte and the main press all reproduce the same table ([EY Tax Snapshots](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-my/insights/tax/malaysia-budget-2027/documents/ey-tax-snapshots-of-budget-2027.pdf); [Skrine](https://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/october-2026/tax-measures-under-the-2027-malaysian-budget); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/09/budget-2027-lower-income-tax-for-rm70000-to-rm150000-range)). The only differences are loose band labels such as "RM70,000 to RM100,000". On the rates alone, the cut saves at most **RM800 a year**, reached at chargeable income of RM150,000. Adding the RM3,000 relief gives **RM840 at RM100,000 and RM1,520 at RM150,000**. Deloitte's estimates match this exactly ([The Edge, 9 Oct 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/821289)), and so does Malay Mail's arithmetic ([Malay Mail, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/16/budget-2027-how-much-tax-could-malaysians-save-under-the-proposed-new-rates-we-did-the-maths-for-you/239089)). By our arithmetic, the combined saving peaks near **RM1,640** for pre-relief chargeable income of roughly RM603,000 to RM1 million, which is where the speech's "up to RM1,600" comes from. It then falls and turns negative at about **RM1.085 million**, where the new 30% band costs more than the cuts save. At RM2 million and above, the rate change alone costs RM19,200.

The other reliefs keep their caps but change in scope, and two of the changes regroup items in ways a calculator must model. Medical relief is renamed "Kesejahteraan Kesihatan" and stays at RM10,000. It gains a **postnatal-care sub-limit of RM3,000** and absorbs the standalone RM1,000 breastfeeding-equipment relief, which can now be claimed whether self or spouse paid. Education becomes "Yuran Pengajian dan Latihan Kemahiran", still capped at **RM7,000**. It now covers any field from certificate to PhD, has a **TVET sub-limit of RM3,000** for self or spouse, and a **RM2,000 sub-limit** for non-work courses or children's STEM, arts, language and religion classes; the course item formerly under lifestyle moves here. Lifestyle stays at RM2,500 and adds AI subscriptions, shelter pet adoption, and vet vaccination and neutering. Sports stays at RM1,000 and adds sports shoes up to RM300. Parents-and-grandparents relief stays at RM8,000 but covers all care expenses, and doctor certification is no longer needed. Tourism relief of RM1,000 is extended for **YA2027 only**. SOCSO/EIS relief stays at RM350, now also covering mandatory LINDUNG KENDIRI, plus a **new RM150 limit for voluntary LINDUNG 24 and non-mandatory LINDUNG KENDIRI contributions** ([MOF Langkah Cukai, Lampiran 3–9](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/ucapan/langkah-cukai.pdf)). One vendor summary claimed a merged RM7,000 education limit, and EY says "relief amounts remain unchanged". Both are consistent with the official text: the cap is unchanged, and the new pieces are sub-limits inside it.

The budget is equally notable for what it leaves alone. None of the 36 tax-measure appendices touches the **RM400 self and spouse rebates, the RM35,000 threshold, the zakat or departure-levy rebates**, or the spouse, child, EPF/life-insurance, childcare, disability, SSPN or PRS reliefs ([MOF Langkah Cukai, Senarai Lampiran](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/ucapan/langkah-cukai.pdf)). That conclusion rests on the measures being absent, not on any explicit confirmation. On payroll, the speech raises the **minimum wage from RM1,700 to RM2,000 from June 2027**, with SMEs whose sales are under RM50 million exempt ([MOF Ucapan, para 32](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/ucapan/ub27.pdf)). It announces no change to EPF, SOCSO or EIS contribution rates and no change to the MTD formula ([activpayroll, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.activpayroll.com/news-articles/malaysia-budget-2027-proposed-payroll-tax-and-employment-changes)).

## Nothing is law until a Finance Act lands, probably on 31 December

The rate bands sit in Schedule 1 of the Income Tax Act 1967 and self relief in s.46(1)(a), so only an Act of Parliament can change them. Exemption orders cannot. The only Budget 2027 bill to have moved is the **Supply Bill 2027**, the spending bill. It passed its policy stage by voice vote on 29 October 2026, with committee stage running 2–26 November ([The Edge, 29 Oct 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/823823)). Searches of the main Malaysian outlets found no first reading of the Finance Bill as of 6 November. Tax advisers and payroll vendors uniformly describe the measures as proposals "subject to the enactment of the relevant legislation" ([Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/10/malaysia-budget-2027-tax-highlights); [Panda HRMS](https://pandahrms.com/budget-2027-individual-income-tax-relief-changes-ya2027/)). This "not yet tabled" finding is not conclusive: the Parliament bills portal could not be reached, so a very recent first reading cannot be ruled out.

Recent cycles have followed a tight, repeatable timetable, and that makes the likely date predictable:

| Budget | Speech | Finance Bill 1st reading | Dewan Rakyat | Royal assent | Gazetted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 18 Oct 2024 | 19 Nov 2024 | 4 Dec 2024 | 24 Dec 2024 | 31 Dec 2024 (Act 862) |
| 2026 | 10 Oct 2025 | 18 Nov 2025 | 1 Dec 2025 | 27 Dec 2025 | 31 Dec 2025 (Act 874) |
| 2027 | 9 Oct 2026 | not yet found | – | – | likely ~31 Dec 2026 |

Sources: [Finance Act 2024 PDF](https://moore.com.my/taxflash/Finance-Act-2024.pdf); [Act 874, Finance Act 2025](https://lom.agc.gov.my/ilims/upload/portal/akta/outputaktap/3276537_BI/Act%20874%20-%20FINANCE%20ACT%202025.pdf); [Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/two-key-tax-bills-tabled-for-first-reading-in-parliament-A2511_UHu3XL); [Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/dewan-rakyat-passes-finance-bill-2025-with-llp-tax-changes-A2512_sQNXdC).

If the pattern holds, the bill should get its first reading in mid-to-late November 2026, pass the Dewan Rakyat in early December, receive royal assent in late December and be **gazetted around 31 December 2026**, just before YA2027 begins. That is a projection, not a schedule. If the timetable slips, the measures would still cover the whole of YA2027, because Finance Acts tie each amendment to a year of assessment ("for the year of assessment 2027 and subsequent years"), as Act 874 did. Material change before enactment looks unlikely. KPMG reports that Finance Act 2025 was gazetted with "no material changes" from its bill ([KPMG Malaysia](https://kpmg.com/my/en/home/insights/2026/01/gazette-of-the-finance-and-tax-acts-2025.html)), the government passed the Supply Bill on a voice vote, and no source reports opposition to the individual measures. The headline numbers (RM12,000, 18%/24%, 30% above RM1 million) are therefore the safest part. The detailed drafting of the reliefs is where the bill is most likely to differ from press summaries: sub-limits, whether the SOCSO RM150 is pooled with the RM350, and the exact s.46 paragraph numbers.

PCB follows its own trigger. LHDN's employer page still lists only the **2026** MTD specification, the TP1/TP3 forms and the testing questions ([HASiL, Jadual PCB dan Spesifikasi Data](https://www.hasil.gov.my/en/majikan/jadual-pcb-dan-spesifikasi-data/)). The 2026 specification was dated "Updated: 01 January 2026", the day after Act 874 was gazetted, and kept D at RM9,000 ([LHDN PCB spec 2026](https://www.hasil.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/spesifikasi-kaedah-pengiraan-berkomputer-pcb-2026.pdf)). That single data point suggests a 2027 spec in late December 2026 or early January 2027. Unlike 2026, when the specification said "there is no amendment to the MTD formula", 2027 must bring a new Table 1, because the bands themselves change. Payroll guides advise employers not to adjust PCB until LHDN publishes. If the spec arrives late, the cumulative MTD formula catches up in later months, and the final liability is settled on the YA2027 return filed in 2028.

## The repo's YA2027 template already matches the official text

The repo already handles the proposal correctly. `docs/research/data/tax-ya2027-proposed.json` carries `status: "unconfirmed-template"` and `taxStatus: "proposed"`, with stages proposed → bill-tabled → enacted. Its eleven brackets are identical to Lampiran 1, and its tax-at-lower-bound values (3,700 / 9,100 / 21,100 / 83,600 / 135,600 / 247,600) are correctly labelled as computed, not published. It also records RM12,000 self relief and the Lampiran 3–9 scope changes, including breastfeeding merged into medical, tourism for YA2027 only, and SOCSO at 350 + 150 with pooling marked unconfirmed. `docs/research/data/pcb-spec-2027-template.json` holds a Table 1 that we derived independently and checked row by row. Its D is 12,000; S, Du, Su and Q are unchanged; and its formula and rounding are assumed unchanged from 2026. `docs/research/pcb.md` §4.1–4.5 and §6.3 describe the same template, with confidence rated "Low to medium: flag in UI". `docs/research/v1-baseline.md` decision D6 keeps 2027 on 2026 values until the figures are confirmed.

The cross-check against the official speech annex ([ub27.pdf Lampiran 1–9](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/ucapan/ub27.pdf)) found **no factual disagreement on any Budget 2027 item**: brackets, self relief, and all relief scope changes. The repo's RM1.5 million cross-check (RM388,400 for YA2026, RM397,600 for YA2027) also reproduces. Where secondary sources disagree, the repo already follows the primary text. One example is the press reports that attach the RM150 to *mandatory* LINDUNG KENDIRI, which Lampiran 9's "caruman sukarela" contradicts. Three weak spots remain. The per-relief `confidence` and `source` fields in `tax-ya2027-proposed.json` are null. The MOF English annex (`tax-measures.pdf`) could not be re-read in this pass because its text would not extract. And every item carried forward from YA2026 is unconfirmed: rebates, non-resident 30%, EPF/life-insurance, spouse, child and the others. The budget's silence on them is not proof that nothing changes.

## Middle earners gain most; RM2,000 earners gain nothing

The figures below are **illustrative, proposed and not law**. They use the repo's v1 method: chargeable income is 12 × gross, less the EPF relief (capped at RM4,000), less self relief; the RM400 rebate applies where chargeable income is RM35,000 or less; and PCB is annual tax ÷ 12. EPF, SOCSO (including SKBBK) and EIS are held at 2026 values. The YA2026 column reproduces `docs/research/data/v1-baseline.py`, and the YA2027 tax equals the `pcb-spec-2027-template.json` formula at every salary. Brackets and relief come from [ub27.pdf Lampiran 1–2](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/ucapan/ub27.pdf).

| Gross/month | Chargeable income 26 → 27 | Annual tax 26 → 27 | PCB/month 26 → 27 | Take-home/month 26 → 27 | Gain/month | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RM2,000 | 12,360 → 9,360 | 0 → 0 | 0.00 → 0.00 | 1,751.70 → 1,751.70 | **0.00** | Rebate already wipes out tax |
| RM4,000 | 35,000 → 32,000 | 200 → 110 | 16.67 → 9.17 | 3,486.03 → 3,493.53 | **7.50** | RM3,000 × 3% |
| RM5,000 | 47,000 → 44,000 | 1,320 → 1,140 | 110.00 → 95.00 | 4,268.20 → 4,283.20 | **15.00** | RM3,000 × 6% |
| RM9,000 | 95,000 → 92,000 | 8,450 → 7,660 | 704.17 → 638.33 | 7,219.53 → 7,285.37 | **65.84** | Relief × 19% (570) + 1-pt cut (220) |
| RM15,000 | 167,000 → 164,000 | 26,150 → 24,600 | 2,179.17 → 2,050.00 | 11,084.53 → 11,213.70 | **129.17** | 1-pt cuts (800) + relief × 25% (750) |

The pattern is clear: below RM70,000 of chargeable income, the relief does all the work, and its value is RM3,000 times the marginal rate. Only above about RM7,170 a month (where YA2027 chargeable income passes RM70,000 for a single filer with the full RM4,000 EPF relief) does the rate cut add anything, and at RM15,000 it is worth more than the relief. Two details matter for the calculator. First, at RM4,000 a real LHDN payslip would show **0.00 in January and 10.00 from February to December**, not 9.17 a month, because MTD under RM10 is set to zero (pcb.md §6.3). The annual total is the same. Second, the **rebate cliff moves up by about RM250 a month**. The RM35,000 threshold is unchanged, but the extra RM3,000 of relief shifts the point where a single 30-year-old on Part A EPF loses the rebate from RM4,000→4,001 to **RM4,250→4,251**. The size of the jump is unchanged: PCB rises about RM33.39. Anyone earning between roughly RM4,001 and RM4,250 a month gains about **RM490 a year** (the RM400 rebate regained plus RM90), far more than the 3%-band headline suggests. That is our arithmetic, and it is not law. The figures exclude the June 2027 minimum-wage rise, which would raise gross pay, EPF and SOCSO for workers at RM1,700–2,000, and they exclude optional TP1 reliefs such as SOCSO RM350 + RM150.

## Three separate triggers gate the calculator update

Per the owner's instruction, nothing has been changed. The work splits by trigger, because the tax law, the PCB specification and the minimum wage come from different instruments on different dates.

**When the Finance Act is gazetted (tax law):**

- `tax-ya2027-proposed.json`:
  - Set `taxStatus` to "enacted" and record the Act number, assent date and gazette date.
  - Re-check each bracket and relief against the Act text, especially the sub-limits (postnatal RM3,000, breastfeeding RM1,000, TVET RM3,000, RM2,000 courses/tuition, sports shoes RM300) and whether the SOCSO RM150 is separate from or pooled with the RM350.
  - Resolve every "carried forward (unconfirmed)" item: RM400 self and spouse rebates, the RM35,000 threshold, non-resident 30%, spouse/child/EPF/life-insurance/SSPN/PRS and the other reliefs.
  - Fill the per-relief `source` and `confidence` fields.
  - Rename the file or record, since it would no longer be "proposed".
- `pcb.md`: update §4.1, §4.2, §4.4, §4.5 and §10 to mark YA2027 as enacted.
- `v1-baseline.md` §5 and decision D6, and `v1-baseline.py`: switch the 2027 tax brackets and self relief from 2026 values to the enacted YA2027 values for annual tax.
- If the calculator shows the rebate-cliff threshold, move it (about RM4,000 → about RM4,250 single).

**When LHDN publishes the 2027 MTD specification (PCB):**

- `pcb-spec-2027-template.json`:
  - Replace the derived Table 1 B values with the published ones, and change `status`, `specStatus` and `bSource`.
  - Confirm D = 12,000, S, Du, Su and Q, the formula and the rounding rules.
  - Map the new TP1 2027 relief lines.
- Add LHDN's "MTD Testing Question 2027" cases to `pcb-verification-cases.json`.
- Recompute `pcb.md` §6.2 thresholds and §6.3 results.
- Record the month from which employers apply it.

**When the Minimum Wages Order for RM2,000 is gazetted** (a separate instrument, not the Finance Act):

- Update the EPF wage floor (`MIN_WAGE_FLOOR` in `v1-baseline.py`, and the v1-baseline §5 row) with its exact June 2027 start.

## Conclusion

Budget 2027 is a relief story more than a rate story for the calculator's main users. For a single filer under about RM7,170 a month, the whole benefit comes from the RM3,000 relief, worth at most a few tens of ringgit a month. The more interesting effect sits at the edge of the RM35,000 rebate: a narrow band of earners around RM4,000–4,250 gains several times the headline amount because they win back the RM400 rebate. The calculator should state that explicitly rather than let a small headline number hide it. The rate cut matters only from the RM70,000 band up, and the new 30% band makes the package a tax rise above about RM1.085 million.

The repo's main risk is not the numbers. Its proposal figures are verified against the primary text. The risk is a gap in sequencing: gazettement around 31 December and LHDN's specification around 1 January may arrive days apart, and the calculator should not show an enacted annual tax next to unpublished PCB values without saying which is which. Treating the Act, the MTD specification and the Minimum Wages Order as three independent status flags keeps the "do not change until gazetted" rule clean when the trigger dates do not line up.
