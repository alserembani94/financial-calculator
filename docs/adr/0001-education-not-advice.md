# Education only: no advice, no products

The app explains how a person's finances work and compares their figures with published benchmarks; it never tells them what to do and never names, ranks or recommends a financial product (such as a fund, ASB, PRS, insurance or a bank). In Malaysia, analysing someone's circumstances and giving them a plan can be licensed "financial planning" even when free, and recommending a class of investment can be licensed "investment advice", so staying on the education side keeps the app outside both licences and keeps it neutral. A disclaimer alone doesn't make advice into education, so the wording and features themselves must stay factual: "your EPF would be about RM X; EPF's Basic Savings for your age is RM Y", never "you should". The app also takes no referral, affiliate or product-placement income, since that can make education count as a business of advice.

## Consequences

- Features that would cross the line are out of scope: personalised plans, product comparisons, "best" choices, and AI that answers personal "what should I do" questions.
- There's no overall financial health rating. Each measure gets its own signal against its benchmark instead, because a single verdict on the person comes closest to licensed financial planning, and people tend to stop looking at their finances when told they're doing badly.
- Each feature's wording is checked against this rule, and a Malaysian lawyer should confirm the line before launch (questions in `docs/research/reports/Malaysia personal finance and advice line.md`).
